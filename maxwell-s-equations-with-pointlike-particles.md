<h1 id="maxwell-s-equations-with-pointlike-particles">Maxwell's equations with pointlike particles</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solutions of Maxwell's equations](solutions-of-maxwell-s-equations.md)

In the standard formulation of [Maxwell's equations](maxwell-s-equations.md), the [electric current](electric-current.md) is a convient but magic input.

Would it be possible to use [Maxwell's equations](maxwell-s-equations.md) to solve a system of [pointlike particles](point-particle.md) such as electrons instead?

The following suggest no, or only for certain subcases less general than [Maxwell's equations](maxwell-s-equations.md):
- [https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/498892/solution-to-maxwell-lorentz-equations](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/498892/solution-to-maxwell-lorentz-equations)
- [https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/380741/complete-classical-description-of-two-interacting-charges](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/380741/complete-classical-description-of-two-interacting-charges)
This is the type of thing where the probability aspect of [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md) seems it could "help".
