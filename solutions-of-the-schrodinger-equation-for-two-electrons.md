This is closely linked to the [Pauli exclusion principle](pauli-exclusion-principle.md).

What does a particle even mean, right? Especially in [quantum field theory](quantum-field-theory-split.md), where two electrons are just vibrations of a single electron field.

Another issue is that if we consider [magnetism](magnetism.md), things only make sense if we add [special relativity](special-relativity.md), since [Maxwell's equations require special relativity](maxwell-s-equations-require-special-relativity.md), so a non approximate solution for this will necessarily require full [quantum electrodynamics](quantum-electrodynamics.md).

As mentioned at [lecture 1](david-tong-s-2009-quantum-field-theory-lectures-at-the-perimeter-institute/lecture-1.md) [https://youtube.com/watch?video=H3AFzbrqH68&t=555](https://youtube.com/watch?video=H3AFzbrqH68&t=555), [relativistic](special-relativity.md) quantum mechanical theories like the [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md) and [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md) make no sense for a "single particle": they must imply that particles can pop in out of existence.
