###### Argonne National Laboratory

↑ **Parent:** [United States Department of Energy national laboratory](#united-states-department-of-energy-national-laboratory)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory)

This is where they moved the [Chicago Pile-1](nuclear-weapon.md#chicago-pile-1) after they decided it might be a bad idea to run [highly experimental nuclear reactions](nuclear-weapon.md#chicago-pile-1) right in the middle of one of the most populous cities of the [United States](united-states.md).

After it was reassembled, the [Chicago Pile-1](nuclear-weapon.md#chicago-pile-1) was renamed as Chicago Pile 2 (CP2).

So more precisely, it is a continuation of the [Metallurgical Laboratory](nuclear-weapon.md#metallurgical-laboratory).

It's still not that far though, only about 20 kilometers, and today is also a populated area.

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) maintains that they chose the site because the name is so cool. Wikipedia says it is derived from the [Forest of Argonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_Argonne), maybe it even shared etymology with the element [argon](chemistry.md#argon).

##### Max Planck Society

↑ **Parent:** [German physics research institute](#german-physics-research-institute)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society)

A massive group of research institutes scattered across [Germany](continent.md#germany). The constituent institutes are known as "[Max Planck Institutes](#max-planck-institute) (for|of) X". They are primarily known for their incredible [natural science](science.md#natural-science) research, but they also cover [social sciences](science.md#social-science).
