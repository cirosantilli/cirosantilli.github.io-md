# Position and momentum space

↑ **Parent:** [Uncertainty principle](uncertainty-principle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_and_momentum_space)

One of the main reasons why physicists are obsessed by this topic is that position and momentum are mapped to the [phase space coordinates](phase-space-coordinate.md) of [Hamiltonian mechanics](hamiltonian-mechanics.md), which appear in the [matrix mechanics](matrix-mechanics.md) formulation of [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md), which offers insight into the theory, particularly when generalizing to [relativistic quantum mechanics](relativistic-quantum-mechanics-split.md).

One way to think is: what is the definition of space space? It is a way to write the wave function $\psi_x(x)$ such that:
- the position operator is the multiplication by $x$
- the momentum operator is the derivative by $x$
And then, what is the definition of momentum space? It is of course a way to write the wave function $\psi_p(p)$ such that:
- the momentum operator is the multiplication by $p$

[https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/39442/intuitive-explanation-of-why-momentum-is-the-fourier-transform-variable-of-posit/39508#39508](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/39442/intuitive-explanation-of-why-momentum-is-the-fourier-transform-variable-of-posit/39508#39508) gives the best idea intuitive idea: the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform.md) writes a function as a (continuous) sum of plane waves, and each plane wave has a fixed momentum.
