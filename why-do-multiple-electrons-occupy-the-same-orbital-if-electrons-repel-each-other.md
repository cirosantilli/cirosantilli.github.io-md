# Why do multiple electrons occupy the same orbital if electrons repel each other?

↑ **Parent:** [Hartree-Fock method](hartree-fock-method.md)

That is, two electrons per [atomic orbital](atomic-orbital.md), each with a different [spin](spin-physics.md).

As shown at [Schrödinger equation solution for the helium atom](schrodinger-equation-solution-for-the-helium-atom.md), they do repel each other, and that affects their measurable energy.

However, this energy is still lower than going up to the next orbital. TODO numbers.

- [https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/505263/do-electrons-in-the-same-orbital-but-different-spin-feel-each-others-coulomb-re](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/505263/do-electrons-in-the-same-orbital-but-different-spin-feel-each-others-coulomb-re)

This changes however at higher orbitals, notably as approximately described by the [aufbau principle](aufbau-principle.md).
