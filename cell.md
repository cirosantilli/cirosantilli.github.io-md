### Apoptosis

↑ **Parent:** [Cell cycle](#cell-cycle)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Anti-cancer mechanism](biology.md#anti-cancer-mechanism)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis)

[Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) part 5 "Murder or suicide" mentions that apoptosis has two main functions:
- prevent [cancer](biology.md#cancer)
- but it is also an important part of [body development](taxonomy.md#developmental-biology), e.g. fingers are separated because cells between them kill themselves. Otherwise we would have webbed feet like ducks. There is also a lot of apoptosis during the [development of the brain](taxonomy.md#developmental-neurobiology). This has been observed notably in [C. elegans](taxonomy.md#caenorhabditis-elegans) see also: [Section "C. elegans cell lineage"](taxonomy.md#c-elegans-cell-lineage).

#### Apoptosis is largely regulated by mitochondria

↑ **Parent:** [Apoptosis](#apoptosis)

[Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) part 5 "Murder or suicide" mentions that the key events that leads to [apoptosis](#apoptosis) is when certain proteins normally present in the [inner mitochondrial membrane](#inner-mitochondrial-membrane) spill out, and that this often happens when [free radicals](molecular-biology.md#free-radical) are produced in excess: the cell is really not doing well in those cases. This point suggests that the initial [mitochondrial endosymbiosis](#mitochondrial-endosymbiosis) happened due to a parasite that lived inside another cell. It mentions that even today we see parasites kill the host cell when they feel that the cell does not have many nutrients. This frees the parasites to then infect other cells.

##### Mitochondrial DNA

↑ **Parent:** [Mitochondrion](#mitochondrion)

<h6 id="key-mitochondrial-proteins-aren-t-necessarily-in-mtdna">Key mitochondrial proteins aren't necessarily in mtDNA</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Mitochondrial DNA](#mitochondrial-dna)

E.g. in [humans](human.md) the [adenine nucleotide translocator](#adenine-nucleotide-translocator) is present in chromosome 4, not in [mtDNA](#mitochondrial-dna).

These have almost certainly been transferred to nuclear DNA in the course of evolution.

This isn't completely surprising, since when mitochondria die, their DNA is kind of left in the cell, so it is not hard to imagine how genes end up getting uptaken by the nucleus. This is suggested at [Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) page 196.

A limiting factor appears to be that you can't just past those genes in the nucleus, further mutations are necessary for [mitochondrial protein import](#mitochondrial-protein-import) to work, apparenty some kind of tagging with extra [amino acids](protein.md#amino-acid).

However, you likely don't want to remove all genes from the [mitochondria](#mitochondrion) because [mitochondria have DNA because they need to be controlled individually](#mitochondria-have-dna-because-they-need-to-be-controlled-individually).

###### Mitochondrial protein import

↑ **Parent:** [Key mitochondrial proteins aren't necessarily in mtDNA](#key-mitochondrial-proteins-aren-t-necessarily-in-mtdna)

The process that imports proteins encoded in the nuclear DNA and made in the [cytosol](#cytosol) into the [mitochondria](#mitochondrion).

The term is mentioned e.g. in this article: [https://www.nature.com/articles/nrm2959](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrm2959).

[Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) suggests that proteins are somehow tagged with extra [amino acids](protein.md#amino-acid) for this.

###### Mitochondria have DNA because they need to be controlled individually

↑ **Parent:** [Key mitochondrial proteins aren't necessarily in mtDNA](#key-mitochondrial-proteins-aren-t-necessarily-in-mtdna)

Argued at [Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) page 212.

Basically, energy supply has to be modulated rather quickly, because we spend a lot sometimes, and very little other times.

Even not turning it off quickly enough is a problem, as it starts to generate [free radicals](molecular-biology.md#free-radical) which fuck you up.

If control came from the nucleus, it has no way to address different mitochondria. But it might be that only one of the mitochondria needs the change. If the nucleus tells all mitochondria to stop producing when only one is full, the others are going to say: "nope, I'm not full, continue producing!" and the one that need to stop will have its signal overridden by the others.

###### Mitochondrial DNA mutates faster than nuclear DNA

↑ **Parent:** [Key mitochondrial proteins aren't necessarily in mtDNA](#key-mitochondrial-proteins-aren-t-necessarily-in-mtdna)

Wikipedia mentions "Since animal mtDNA evolves faster than nuclear genetic markers" with a few sources.

Some sources:
- [Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) page 361 mentions:> While nuclear DNA can barely distinguish between chimps and humans, the mitochondrial clock ticks fast enough to reveal differences accumulating over tens of thousands of years

  so this property is also important for the [human mitochondrial molecular clock](human.md#human-mitochondrial-molecular-clock).

##### Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)

> As it happens, nearly 15 years have passed since the 1st edition of Power, Sex, Suicide was published, and I am resisting the temptation to make any lame revisions. Some say that even Darwin lessened the power of his arguments in the Origin of Species through his multiple revisions, in which he dealt with criticisms and sometimes shifted his views in the wrong direction. I prefer my original to speak for itself, even if it turns out to be wrong.

- [there are two sexes because of mitochondria](biology.md#there-are-two-sexes-because-of-mitochondria)

  [There are no known eukaryotes which never had mitochondria](#there-are-no-known-eukaryotes-which-never-had-mitochondria). Having mitochondria appears to be a requisite for being an eukaryote.

  Contrast this for example with [multicellularity](taxonomy.md#multicellular-organism), which [is highly polyphyletic](taxonomy.md#multicellularity-is-polyphyletic).
- [Apoptosis is largely regulated by mitochondria](#apoptosis-is-largely-regulated-by-mitochondria)
- there are two main theories for how the [mitochondria](#mitochondrion) [endosymbiosis](#endosymbiont) started:
  - [parsitic hypothesis of mitochondrial endosymbiosis](#parsitic-hypothesis-of-mitochondrial-endosymbiosis): a parasitic option rather than cooperative
  - [hydrogen hypothesis](#hydrogen-hypothesis): a cooperative option rather than parasitic

Rather, they are [dictatorships](social-technology.md#dictatorship), where any dissident is forced to commit [seppuku](japan.md#seppuku). [Lu Xun's petition quote](https://cirosantilli.com/china-dictatorship/what-should-pro-democracy-chinese-living-in-china-do-about-the-dictatorship#lu-xun-petition) comes to mind.

Nitpicks:
- the book calls [ATP synthase](#atp-synthase) "ATPase" in several points, which is confusing because -ase means "something that breaks", and in 2020 parlance, there are ATPases which actually break ATP: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATPase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATPase). The book itself acknowledges that on page 135:> The ATPase is freely reversible. Under some circumstances it can go into reverse, whereupon it splits ATP, and uses the energy released to pump protons up the drive shaft, back across the membrane against the pressure of the reservoir. In fact the very name ATPase (rather than ATP synthase) signifies this action, which was discovered first. This bizarre trait hides a deep secret of life, and we’ll return to it in a moment.

Some criticisms:
- some of the later chapters are a bit more boring, like the stuff about [warm-blooded](taxonomy.md#warm-blooded) animals. Perhaps is it that [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) is more interested in the molecular aspects than macro
- the author talks about some very recent research at the time. While this does highlight his expertise, some of the points mentioned might still be in a state of flow. This is acknowledged by the author himself on the 2018 updated preface however.

###### Mitochondrial phosphate carrier protein

↑ **Parent:** [Mitochondrial carrier](#mitochondrial-carrier)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Transmembrane protein](protein.md#transmembrane-protein)

Just like the [adenine nucleotide translocator](#adenine-nucleotide-translocator) moves [ATP](#adenosine-triphosphate)/[ADP](#adenosine-diphosphate) in and out, this one moves loose [phosphate](chemistry.md#phosphate) in.

Both of those together recycle the cellular respiration carriers from/to the mitochondria.

The fact that ATP is the universal energy storage mollecule of all life on Earth is such an incredible unifying principle of biology!

It is the direct output of all the major forms of "energy generation" in cells: [ATP synthesis mechanism](#atp-synthesis-mechanism).

It is just as fundamental as the [genetic code](dna.md#genetic-code) for example.

No wonder dozens of [Nobel Prizes](nobel-prize.md) were related to its discovery, given its complexity.

###### ATP synthase

↑ **Parent:** [ATP synthesis mechanism](#atp-synthesis-mechanism)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Cellular respiration protein](#cellular-respiration-protein), [Protein complex](protein.md#protein-complex), [Transmembrane protein](protein.md#transmembrane-protein)

One of the most beautiful [molecular machines](technology.md#molecular-machine) known!

The first one with such complexity that was uncovered.

The thing rotates like a water wheel for God's sake, except it uses [protons](standard-model.md#proton) instead of water.

The ATP synthase complex is so large that [Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) page 123 contains a [cryoEM](microscopy.md#cryogenic-electron-microscopy) image of several [ATP synthases](#atp-synthase) on small membrane vesicles, this is the paper: [https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/bi00437a031#](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/bi00437a031#) under [a fucking paywall](education.md#closed-access-academic-journals-are-evil).
