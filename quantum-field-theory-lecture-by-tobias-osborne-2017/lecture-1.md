Course outline given:
- classical field theory
- quantum scalar field. Covers [bosons](../boson.md), and is simpler to get intuition about.
- quantum Dirac field. Covers [fermions](../fermion.md)
- interacting fields
- [perturbation theory](../perturbation-theory.md)
- [renormalization](../renormalization.md)

Non-relativistic [QFT](../quantum-field-theory-split.md) is a limit of relativistic QFT, and can be used to describe for example [condensed matter physics](../condensed-matter-physics-split.md) systems at very low temperature. But it is still very hard to make accurate measurements even in those experiments.

Defines "relativistic" as: "the [Lagrangian](../lagrangian.md) is symmetric under the [Poincaré group](../poincare-group.md)".

Mentions that "QFT is hard" because (a finite list follows???):

> There are no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary [representations](../representation-theory.md) of the [Poincaré group](../poincare-group.md).

But I guess that if you fully understand what that means precisely, QTF won't be too hard for you!

Notably, this is stark contrast with rotation symmetry groups ([SO(3)](../special-orthogonal-group.md)) which appears in space rotations present in [non-relativistic quantum mechanics](../non-relativistic-quantum-mechanics.md).
