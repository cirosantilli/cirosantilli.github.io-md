# The beauty of mathematics

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematics](mathematics-split.md)

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli-split.md) intends to move his beauty list here little by little: [https://github.com/cirosantilli/mathematics/blob/master/beauty.md](https://github.com/cirosantilli/mathematics/blob/master/beauty.md)

The most beautiful things in mathematics are results that are:
- [simple to state but hard to prove](simple-to-state-but-hard-to-prove.md):
  - Fermat's Last Theorem
  - [number of unknown rationality](number-of-unknown-rationality.md), e.g. is $e + \pi$ [rational](rational-number.md)?
  - [transcendental number conjectures](transcendental-number-conjecture.md), e.g. is $e + \pi$ transcendental?
  - basically any conjecture involving [prime numbers](prime-number.md):
    - [twin prime conjecture](twin-prime-conjecture.md)
  - many combinatorial game questions, e.g.:
    - [first-move advantage in chess](first-move-advantage-in-chess.md)
- surprising results: we had intuitive reasons to believe something as possible or not, but a theorem shatters that conviction and brings us on our knees, sometimes via [pathological](pathological-mathematics.md) counter-examples. General surprise themes include:
  - [classification](classification-mathematics.md) of potentially [infinite](infinity.md) sets like: compact [manifolds](manifold.md), etc.
    - [classification of finite simple groups](classification-of-finite-simple-groups.md)
    - [classification of regular polytopes](classification-of-regular-polytopes.md)
    - [classification of closed surfaces](classification-of-closed-surfaces.md), and more [generalized Poincaré conjectures](generalized-poincare-conjecture.md)
    - classification of [wallpaper groups](wallpaper-group.md) and [space groups](space-group.md)
  - problems that are more complicated in low dimensions than high like:
    - [generalized Poincaré conjectures](generalized-poincare-conjecture.md). It is also fun to see how in many cases complexity peaks out at 4 dimensions.
    - [classification of regular polytopes](classification-of-regular-polytopes.md)
  - unpredictable magic constants:
    - why is the lowest dimension for an [exotic sphere](exotic-sphere.md) 7?
    - why is 4 the largest degree of an equation with explicit solution? [Abel-Ruffini theorem](abel-ruffini-theorem.md)
  - [undecidable problems](undecidable-problem.md), especially simple to state ones:
    - [mortal matrix problem](mortal-matrix-problem.md)
    - sharp frontiers between solvable and unsolvable are also cool:
      - attempts at determining [specific values of the Busy beaver function](specific-values-of-the-busy-beaver-function.md) for [Turing machines](turing-machine.md) with a given number of states and symbols
      - related to [Diophantine equations](diophantine-equation.md):
        - [decidability of Hilbert's tenth problem of a given degree and number of variables](decidability-of-hilbert-s-tenth-problem-of-a-given-degree-and-number-of-variables.md)
        - [Hilbert's tenth problem over other rings](hilbert-s-tenth-problem-over-other-rings.md)

  Lists:
  - [https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/139699/what-are-some-examples-of-a-mathematical-result-being-counterintuitive](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/139699/what-are-some-examples-of-a-mathematical-result-being-counterintuitive)
  - [https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2040811/what-are-some-counter-intuitive-results-in-mathematics-that-involve-only-finite/2055458#2055458](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2040811/what-are-some-counter-intuitive-results-in-mathematics-that-involve-only-finite/2055458#2055458)
- applications: make life easier and/or modeling some phenomena well, e.g. in [physics](physics-split.md). See also: [explain how to make money with the lesson](how-to-teach/explain-how-to-make-money-with-the-lesson.md)

Good lists of such problems [Lists of mathematical problems](lists-of-mathematical-problems.md).

Whenever [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli-split.md) learns a bit of [mathematics](mathematics-split.md), he always wonders to himself:

> Am I achieving insight, or am I just memorizing definitions?

Unfortunately, due to how man books are written, it is not really possible to reach insight without first doing a bit of memorization. The better the book, the more insight is spread out, and less you have to learn before reaching each insight.
