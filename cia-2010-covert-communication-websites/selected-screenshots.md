# Selected screenshots

↑ **Parent:** [Results](results.md)

<a id="_156"></a>
This section contains some of the most interesting and a few representative screenshots of the websites found.

<a id="_157"></a>
We intentionally omit the screenshots already reported by the [Reuters article](reuters-article.md).

<a id="image-2010-wayback-machine-archive-of-starwarsweb-net"></a>
<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cirosantilli/media/master/cia-2010-covert-communication-websites/screenshots/starwarsweb.net.jpg" alt="" height="1400">

**[Figure 16](#image-2010-wayback-machine-archive-of-starwarsweb-net). 2010 Wayback Machine archive of starwarsweb.net**. <a id="_158"></a>
The [Star Wars](../star-wars.md) one. Clearly branded websites like this are rare, which makes finding them all the much more fun. The [Reuters article](reuters-article.md) had two of them ([Carson](searching-for-carson.md) and rastadirect.net), so these were probably manually selected from the full hit dataset, and did not serve specifically as entry points. Most of the websites are quite boring and forgetful as you'd expect.

<a id="_159"></a>
The subtitle "Beyond The Unknown" may be a reference to the [Unknown Regions](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Unknown_Regions), an unexplored area of the galaxy in the Star Wars fictional universe.

<a id="image-stock-photo-of-a-jedi-boy-from-getty-images-used-on-starwarsweb-net"></a>

**[Figure 17](#image-stock-photo-of-a-jedi-boy-from-getty-images-used-on-starwarsweb-net). Stock photo of a Jedi boy from Getty Images used on starwarsweb.net**. [Source](https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/photo/little-jedi-royalty-free-image/172984439). <a id="_160"></a>
Marked as Uploaded 10 October, 2008.

<a id="_161"></a>
The photo can still be licensed today as of 2025: [https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/photo/little-jedi-royalty-free-image/172984439](https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/photo/little-jedi-royalty-free-image/172984439). We found it by [searching for "jedi boy" on gettyimages.co.uk](https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/search/2/image?phrase=jedi%20boy&sort=best&license=rf%2Crm). The photo is credited to a `madisonwi`, presumably an alias based on the location [Madison, Wisconsin](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/madison-wisconsin). Here's a random website about adoption that uses it: [https://www.adoptionadvocates.net/star-wars-adoption-language/](https://www.adoptionadvocates.net/star-wars-adoption-language/) and where it can be seen without the watermarks.

<a id="_162"></a>
It later ocurred to [Ciro Santilli](../ciro-santilli-split.md) that perhaps Reuters chose not to showcase this website because it features the photograph of a minor. But Ciro is sure that that minor is now a handsome young man in his 20's and would find the entire story very amusing if he ever finds out about it!

<a id="_163"></a>
The images of the droids can be seen e.g. at: [https://www.amazon.co.uk/04-Kampf-Droiden-Superheftig-Jedi/dp/B004TINSW6](https://www.amazon.co.uk/04-Kampf-Droiden-Superheftig-Jedi/dp/B004TINSW6), a promotional material for a 2008 The Clone Wars television series audio CD and available as transparent PNGs without background in several sources. The Yoda art also seems to come from that show: [https://rpggamer.org/page.php?page=4229](https://rpggamer.org/page.php?page=4229).

<a id="_164"></a>
One can almost picture the contractor who made that site seeing his children watching that show or playing the video game one evening, when a lightbulb popped over their head: tomorrow I'm going to have some fun at work.

<a id="_165"></a>
In retrospect however, this website was likely a bad idea, since the massive pop culture appeal of [Star Wars](../star-wars.md) meant that when [The CIA Secretly Ran a Star Wars Fan Site by Joseph Cox](the-cia-secretly-ran-a-star-wars-fan-site-by-joseph-cox.md) was published in 2025 the combination of "[CIA](../central-intelligence-agency.md)" and "[Star Wars](../star-wars.md)" on a single sentence produced an irresistible clickbait that amplified knowledge of the fiasco to general public in a way that poor Johnny Carson and Bob Marley could never do. That's why you just can't have fun while working for the [secret services](../secret-service.md) anymore.

<img src="https://github.com/cirosantilli/media/blob/master/cia-2010-covert-communication-websites/screenshots/alljohnny.com-2011.jpg?raw=true" alt="" height="715">

**[Figure 18](#image-2011-wayback-machine-archive-of-alljohnny-com). 2011 Wayback Machine archive of alljohnny.com**. [Source](https://web.archive.org/web/20040113025122/http://alljohnny.com/). Although alljohnny.com is one of the original [Reuters examples](the-reuters-websites.md), we are highlighting this screenshot here because the Reuters provided screenshot is from the extremely early [2004 version of the site](https://web.archive.org/web/20040113025122/http://alljohnny.com/), and it is interesting to see how this unique example was later updated in this 2011 version, the only known such case so far. The lack of OPSEC awareness is mind blowing, them reusing a domain like that after so many years in a completely new threat environment and possibly for a new asset.
