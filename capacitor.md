# Capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Electronic component](electronic-component.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor)

The fundamental intuition about capacitors is that they never let [electrons](electron.md) through.

They can only absorb [electrons](electron.md) up to a certain point, but then the pushback becomes too strong, and current stops.

Therefore, they cannot conduct [direct current](direct-current.md) long term.

For [alternating current](alternating-current.md) however, things are different, because in alternating current, [electrons](electron.md) are just jiggling back and forward a little bit around a center point. So you can send alternating current power across a capacitor.

The key equation that relates [Voltage](voltage.md) to [electric current](electric-current.md) in the [capacitor](capacitor.md) is:

$$
I(t) = C \dv{V(t)}{t}
$$

So if a voltage [Heavyside step function](heavyside-step-function.md) is applied what happens is:
- the capacitor fills up instantly with an infinite current
- the current then stops instantly
More realistically, one may consider the behavior or the [series RC circuit](series-rc-circuit.md) to see what happens without infinities when a capacitor is involved as in the [step response of the series RC circuit](step-response-of-the-series-rc-circuit.md).
