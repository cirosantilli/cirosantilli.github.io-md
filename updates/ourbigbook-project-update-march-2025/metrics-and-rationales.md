<h1 id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/metrics-and-rationales">Metrics and rationales</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [OurBigBook Project Update March 2025](../ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025.md)

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_360"></a>
Long story short, the project is so far a complete failure on the most important metric: number of regular users, which current sits at exactly one: myself.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_361"></a>
There were notable users who found the project online and who actually tried to use the website for some content and provided extremely valuable feedback:<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_362"></a>

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_363"></a>
- [http://ourbigbook.com/pioyi](http://ourbigbook.com/pioyi)
<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_364"></a>
- [https://ourbigbook.com/sidstuff](https://ourbigbook.com/sidstuff)
Unfortunately after the period of a few weeks they stopped using it to follow their other priorities instead. Which is of course totally fine, however sad.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_365"></a>
I still believe that the [OurBigBook Web](../../ourbigbook-web.md) feature is a significant tech innovation that could make the website go big.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_366"></a>
I also believe that the project gets many fundamentals of braindumping right, notably the infinitely deep table of contents without forced scoping, e.g.:<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_367"></a>

```
- Mathematics
  - Calculus
```
does not make Calculus have an ID orr URL of `mathematics/calculus`, rather it's just `calculus`.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_368"></a>
But there is a fundamental difficulty in reaching critical mass to that self-sustaining point, as people don't seem to be convinced by these logical "my system is better" argument alone, as opposed to having them [Google](../../google-split.md) into stuff they need now and then understand that the project is awesome.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_369"></a>
A closely related critical mass issue is that existing big multiuser knowledge base websites such as [Stack Overflow](../../stack-overflow-split.md) and [Wikipedia](../../wikipedia.md) have a tremendous advantage on [PageRank](../../pagerank.md). No matter how useless a Wikipedia article about something is, it will always be on top of [Google](../../google-split.md) within a week of creation for title hits. And since the main goal of publishing your stuff is to get it seen, it makes much more sense for writers to publish on such existing websites whenever possible, because anywhere else it is way way less likely to be seen by anybody.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_370"></a>
Even I end up writing way more on [Stack Overflow](../../stack-overflow-split.md) than on [OurBigBook](../../ourbigbook.md) as a programmer. But I still believe that there is a value to OurBigBook, for the usual reasons of:<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_371"></a>

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_372"></a>
- it  allows you to organize a more global view of a subject, i.e. a book. Even I write answers on [Stack Overflow](../../stack-overflow-split.md), I also tend to organize links to these answers in a structured ways here, see e.g. big topics such as [SQL](../../sql-split.md)
<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_373"></a>
- [deletionism](../../deletionism.md) and overly narrowness of allowed topics/style

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_374"></a>
Perhaps what saddens me the most is that even on [GitHub](../../github.md) stars/[Twitter](../../twitter.md)/Hacker news terms there is almost no interest in the project despite the fact that I consider that it has innovations, while many other [note taking apps](../../personal-knowledge-base-software.md) as well in the thousands of stars. Maybe I'm just delusional and all the tech that I'm doing is completely useless?

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_375"></a>
Part of the issue is probably linked to the fact that most other note taking apps focus on "help me organize my ideas so I can make more money" and often completely ignore "I want to publish my knowledge", and stuff that helps you make money is always easier to sell and promote.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_376"></a>
OurBigBook on the other hand a huge focus on "I want to publish me knowledge". It aims almost single mindedly in being the best tool ever for that. However this doesn't make money for people, and therefore there are going to be way less potential users.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_377"></a>
I do believe strongly that all it takes is a few users for the project to snowball. For some people, once you start braindumping, it is very addictive, and you never want to stop basically. So with only a few of those we can open large parts of undergrad knowledge to the world. But these people are few, and so far I haven't been able to find even a single one like me, and on top of that convince them that I have created the ultimate system for their knowledge publishing desires.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_378"></a>
Another general lesson is that I should perhaps aimed for greater compatibility with existing systems such as [Obsidian](../../obsidian-software.md). Taking something that many people already know and use can have a huge impact on acceptance. E.g. anything that touches [Obsidian](../../obsidian-software.md) can reach thousands of stars: [https://github.com/KosmosisDire/obsidian-webpage-export](https://github.com/KosmosisDire/obsidian-webpage-export). Note taking apps that aim for "[markdown](../../markdown.md)" compatibility also tend to fare better, even if in the end you inevitably have to extend the Markdown for some of your features. And [WYSIWYG](../../wysiwyg.md), which I want but don't have, is perhaps the ultimate familiarity.

<a id="ourbigbook-project-update-march-2025/_379"></a>
Another issue compared to other platforms is that [OurBigBook](../../ourbigbook.md) just came out late. [Obsidian](../../obsidian-software.md) launched in 2020. [Roam Research](../../roam-research.md) and [Trillium Notes](../../trillium-notes.md) also came earlier. And it is hard to fight the advantage already gained by those on the "I'm going to take some personal notes" area. I do believe however that there a strong separation between "these are my personal notes" and "I want to publish these". Once you decide to publish your knowledge, you immediately start to write in a different way, and it is very hard to convert pre-existing "private" notes into ones suitable for public consumption.
