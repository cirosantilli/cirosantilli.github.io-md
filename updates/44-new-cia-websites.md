<a id="_289"></a>
I found 44 new covert websites made by the CIA around 2010 bringing the total to 397!

<a id="_290"></a>
Most websites were boring as usual, but one was slightly cooler: [webofcheer.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20110207150839/http://webofcheer.com/) is a comedy fansite featuring Johnny Carson, Charles Chaplin, Rowan Atkins (of Mr. Bean fame), The Three Stooges and some other [Americans](../united-states-split.md) no one knows about anymore. There must have been a massive Johnny Carson amongst the contractors at that time, given that we previously also knew about [`alljohnny.com`](https://web.archive.org/web/20040113025122/http://alljohnny.com/), a site dedicated fully to him! Both of these sites also serve as some of the earliest examples we've got so far, dating back to 2004 and 2005.

Another cool discovery is that I found the [Getty Images](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/getty-images) source of the [Jedi](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/jedi) boy on their [Star Wars](../star-wars.md) themed site [starwarsweb.net](https://web.archive.org/web/20101230033220/http://starwarsweb.net/): [https://web.archive.org/web/20101230033220/http://starwarsweb.net/](https://web.archive.org/web/20101230033220/http://starwarsweb.net/) The photo can still be licensed today as of 2025: [https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/photo/little-jedi-royalty-free-image/172984439](https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/detail/photo/little-jedi-royalty-free-image/172984439). I found it by searching for "jedi boy" on [https://gettyimages.co.uk.](https://gettyimages.co.uk.) The photo is credited to username `madisonwi`, presumably an alias of a photographer from [Madison, Wisconsin](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/madison-wisconsin). Inspired by this I [reverse image searched](../reverse-image-search.md) and found the source of many other stock images from other websites, and I pinged their authors whenever I could locate them e.g. [https://x.com/cirosantilli/status/1899750172260806711](https://x.com/cirosantilli/status/1899750172260806711).

<a id="_292"></a>
There were two small advances that led to the discovery of new domains:<a id="_293"></a>

<a id="_294"></a>
- <a id="_295"></a>
  while looking for a way to procrastinate I decided to scrape [https://justdropped.com/drops/](https://justdropped.com/drops/) for fun. That website lists expired domain names and see if it would yield any new results.

  <a id="_296"></a>
  I had already [scrapped other expired domain websites before and used that data](../cia-2010-covert-communication-websites/expired-domain-trackers.md), and I hoped that this one would provide some new domain hits, even though it had very large overlap with the other websites I had scraped domains from previously.

  <a id="_297"></a>
  Such domain name lists tend to contain all SCAM domains in existence, since those inevitably expire once the scammers are caught.
<a id="_298"></a>
- even more importantly, I noticed by chance that I was being too strict on a small part of my fingerprinting which was excluding a few good domains, by removing any hits that had multiple archives of the [Communication mechanism](../cia-2010-covert-communication-websites/communication-mechanism.md)

<a id="_299"></a>
With those two new developments, I then kicked off my pre-existing search pipelines searching for domain names with the word `news` on them, an amazingly efficient heuristic because many of the websites were disguised as news aggregators, and after a few hours theses new hits emerged. A few of those also led to the discovery of new IPs which then led to new domains.

<a id="_300"></a>
One entirely new IP range was found around [fastnews-online.com](https://web.archive.org/web/20090420131359/http://fastnews-online.com/) from 208.93.112.105 to 208.93.112.125. There were many domain names with very promising names in the range, but unfortunately for some reason most didn't have [Wayback Machine](../wayback-machine.md) Archives so I didn't count them as hits as per my guidelines.
