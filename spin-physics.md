# Spin (physics)

↑ **Parent:** [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md)

Spin is one of the [defining properties of elementary particles](defining-properties-of-elementary-particles.md), i.e. number that describes how an [elementary particle](elementary-particle.md) behaves, much like [electric charge](electric-charge.md) and [mass](mass.md).

Possible values are half integer numbers: 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2, and so on.

The approach shown in this section: [Section "Spin comes naturally when adding relativity to quantum mechanics"](spin-number-of-a-field.md) shows what the spin number actually means in general. As shown there, the spin number it is a direct consequence of having the laws of nature be [Lorentz invariant](lorentz-invariant.md). Different spin numbers are just different ways in which this can be achieved as per different [Representation of the Lorentz group](representation-of-the-lorentz-group.md).

[Video 8. "Quantum Mechanics 9a - Photon Spin and Schrodinger's Cat I by ViaScience (2013)"](#video-quantum-mechanics-9a-photon-spin-and-schrodinger-s-cat-i-by-viascience-2013) explains nicely how:
- incorporated into the [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md) as a natural consequence of [special relativity](special-relativity.md) corrections, but not naturally present in the [Schrödinger equation](schrodinger-equation.md), see also: [the Dirac equation predicts spin](the-dirac-equation-predicts-spin.md)
- [photon](photon-split.md) spin can be either linear or circular
- the linear one can be made from a superposition of circular ones
- straight antennas produce linearly polarized photos, and [Helical antennas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helical_antenna) circularly polarized ones
- a jump between 2s and 2p in an atom changes angular momentum. Therefore, the photon must carry angular momentum as well as energy.
- cannot be classically explained, because even for a very large estimate of the electron size, its surface would have to spin faster than light to achieve that magnetic momentum with the known [electron charge](elementary-charge.md)
- as shown at [Video 5. "Quantum Mechanics 12b - Dirac Equation II by ViaScience (2015)"](solutions-of-the-dirac-equation.md#video-quantum-mechanics-12b-dirac-equation-ii-by-viascience-2015), observers in different [frames of reference](frame-of-reference.md) see different spin states
