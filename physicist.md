### Adolfo Amidei

↑ **Parent:** [Enrico Fermi](#enrico-fermi)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Amazing mentor](education.md#amazing-mentor)

This dude mentored [Enrico Fermi](#enrico-fermi) in [high school](education.md#secondary-school). [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) added some info to [Fermi](#enrico-fermi)'s [Wikipedia](website.md#wikipedia) page at: [https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Enrico_Fermi&type=revision&diff=1050919447&oldid=1049187703](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Enrico_Fermi&type=revision&diff=1050919447&oldid=1049187703) from [Enrico Fermi: physicist by Emilio Segrè (1970)](#enrico-fermi-physicist-by-emilio-segre-1970):

> In 1914, [Fermi](#enrico-fermi), who used to often meet with his father in front of the office after work, met a colleague of his father called [Adolfo Amidei](#adolfo-amidei), who would walk part of the way home with [Alberto](#alberto-fermi) \[Enrico's father\]. Enrico had learned that Adolfo was interested in [mathematics](mathematics.md) and [physics](physics.md) and took the opportunity to ask Adolfo a question about [geometry](geometry.md). Adolfo understood that the young Fermi was referring to [projective geometry](geometry.md#projective-geometry) and then proceeded to give him a book on the subject written by [Theodor Reye](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/theodor-reye). Two months later, Fermi returned the book, having solved all the problems proposed at the end of the book, some of which Adolfo considered difficult. Upon verifying this, Adolfo felt that Fermi was "a prodigy, at least with respect to geometry", and further mentored the boy, providing him with more books on physics and mathematics. Adolfo noted that Fermi had a very good memory and thus could return the books after having read them because he could remember their content very well.

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) really likes guys like this. Given that he does not have the right genetics, conditions and temperance for scientific greatness in this lifetime, [he dreams of one day finding his own Fermi instead](sponsor.md).

#### A New Method of Measuring Nuclear Magnetic Moment

↑ **Parent:** [Work by Isidor Rabi](#work-by-isidor-rabi)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Paper published on Physical Review ](physics.md#paper-published-on-physical-review)

[https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.53.318](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.53.318) 

[Physical Review](physics.md#physical-review) Volume 53, page 318.

Not [paywalled](education.md#paywall) as of 2024! A miracle! It is barely one page long.

This is the paper that contains the first successful report of experimental [nuclear magnetic moment](particle-physics.md#nuclear-magnetic-moment) observation.

They promise more at the end:

> We have tried this experiment with LiC1 and observed the resonance peaks of Li and Cl. The effects are very striking and the resonances sharp (Fig. 1). A full account of this experiment, together with the values of the nuclear moments, will be published when the homogeneous field is recalibrated.

and this promise was fulfilled on the later [The Molecular Beam Resonance Method for Measuring Nuclear Magnetic Moments](#the-molecular-beam-resonance-method-for-measuring-nuclear-magnetic-moments).

<a id="image-figure-1-of-a-new-method-of-measuring-nuclear-magnetic-moment"></a>
![](https://ia600106.us.archive.org/33/items/a-new-method-of-measuring-nuclear-magnetic-moment-fig-1/A_New_Method_of_Measuring_Nuclear_Magnetic_Moment_Fig_1.png)

**[Figure 12](#image-figure-1-of-a-new-method-of-measuring-nuclear-magnetic-moment). Figure 1 of A New Method of Measuring Nuclear Magnetic Moment**. This figure sums it all up: they were measuring the intensity of one side of a [molecular beam](condensed-matter-physics.md#molecular-beam) after a [Stern-Gerlach experiment](relativistic-quantum-mechanics.md#stern-gerlach-experiment).

Then, they vary the constant [magnetic field](electromagnetism.md#magnetic-field) before the splitting, and at the same time apply a fixed radio frequency to the beam.

When the constant magnetic field makes the energy gap match the radio frequency input, [nuclear spin](particle-physics.md#nuclear-magnetic-moment) of many atoms goes to the anti-aligned direction, the beam gets diverted, so the previously detected beam gets weaker.

## Julian Schwinger

↑ **Parent:** [Physicist](physicist.md)

Extremely precocious, borderline [child prodigy](brain.md#child-prodigy), he was reading [Dirac](#paul-dirac) at 13-14 from the library.

He started working at night and sleeping during the moring/early afternoon while he was at university.

He was the type of guy that was so good that he didn't really have to follow the university rules very much. He would get into trouble for not following some stupid requirement, but he was so good that they would just let him get away with it.

Besides [quantum electrodynamics](quantum-field-theory.md#quantum-electrodynamics), Julian worked on [radar](telecommunication.md#radar) at the [Rad Lab](university.md#mit-radiation-laboratory) during [World War II](science.md#world-war-ii), unlike most other top [physicists](physicist.md) who went to [Los Alamos Laboratory](research-institute.md#los-alamos-laboratory) to work on the [atomic bomb](nuclear-weapon.md), and he made important contributions there on calculating the best shape of the parts and so on.

He was known for being very formal mathematically and sometimes hard to understand, in stark contrast to [Feynman](richard-feynman.md) which was much more lose and understandable, especially after [Freeman Dyson](#freeman-dyson) translated him to the masses.

However, [QED and the men who made it: Dyson, Feynman, Schwinger, and Tomonaga by Silvan Schweber (1994)](quantum-field-theory.md#qed-and-the-men-who-made-it-dyson-feynman-schwinger-and-tomonaga-by-silvan-schweber-1994) does emphacise that he was actually also very practical in the sense that he always aimed to obtain definite numbers out of his calculations, and that was not only the case for the [Lamb shift](quantum-field-theory.md#lamb-shift).

#### On a new radioactive substance contained in pitchblende

↑ **Parent:** [Publication by Marie Curie](#publication-by-marie-curie)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Comptes rendus de l'Académie des Sciences Tome 127](education.md#comptes-rendus-de-l-academie-des-sciences-tome-127)

This is the papaer where [Marie Curie](#marie-curie) announced the discovery of [Polonium](chemistry.md#polonium).

Here's a link with [OCR](machine-learning.md#optical-character-recognition) on the [French](continent.md#france) [Wikisource](law.md#wikisource): [https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Pierre_Curie/23](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Pierre_Curie/23). It's from a 1908 collection of works, but it is the exact same paper.

Here's an [English](linguistics.md#english-language) translation: [https://web.lemoyne.edu/~giunta/curiespo.html](https://web.lemoyne.edu/~giunta/curiespo.html)

First a recap of previous work:

> Some minerals containing [uranium](chemistry.md#uranium) and [thorium](chemistry.md#thorium) ([pitchblende](chemistry.md#uraninite), chalcolite, uranite) are very active from the point of view of the emission of [Becquerel rays](#becquerel-s-rays). In a previous work, one of us showed that their activity is even greater than that of [uranium](chemistry.md#uranium) and [thorium](chemistry.md#thorium), and expressed the opinion that this effect was due to some other very active substance contained in small quantities in these minerals.

Note the cute terminology "[Becquerel rays](#becquerel-s-rays)", which were only later understood to be [electromagnetic radiation](photon.md#electromagnetic-radiation) now known as [gamma rays](particle-physics.md#gamma-ray).

Then some more recapitulation of the previously discussed groundbreaking idea that only atom counts matter for [radioactivity](particle-physics.md#radioactive-decay), regardless of their chemical configuration as in [fluorescence](condensed-matter-physics.md#fluorescence):

> The study of compounds of [uranium](chemistry.md#uranium) and [thorium](chemistry.md#thorium) had shown, in fact, that the property of emitting rays which make air conductive and which act on photographic plates is a specific property of uranium and thorium which is found in all compounds of these metals, all the more weakened as the proportion of active metal in the compound is itself lower. The physical state of the substances seems to have a completely secondary importance. Various experiments have shown that the state of mixture of the substances seems to act only by varying the proportion of active bodies and the absorption produced by the inert substances. Certain causes (such as the presence of impurities) which act so powerfully on [phosphorescence](condensed-matter-physics.md#phosphorescence) or [fluorescence](condensed-matter-physics.md#fluorescence) are therefore here completely without action. It therefore becomes very probable that if certain minerals are more active than uranium and thorium, it is because they contain a substance more active than these metals.

Then the key innovation: they used radioactivity measures to guide their purification work:

> We have sought to isolate this substance in [pitchblende](chemistry.md#uraninite), and experience has confirmed the above predictions.
> 
> Our chemical research has been constantly guided by the control of the radiant activity of the products separated at each operation. Each product is placed on one of the plates of a condenser, and the conductivity acquired by the air is measured using an electrometer and a [piezoelectric](condensed-matter-physics.md#piezoelectric-motor) [quartz](chemistry.md#quartz), as in the work cited above. We thus have not only an indication but a number which accounts for the richness of the product in active substance.

Next they describe in high level their [separation process](chemistry.md#separation-process), and I can't understand anything. But that's OK

Finally towards the end, bombs are dropped:
- the new elemnt is next to [bismuth](chemistry.md#bismuth). If you stop now and look at a [periodic table](chemistry.md#periodic-table), you will see that [bismuth](chemistry.md#bismuth) is exactly one element before [polonium](chemistry.md#polonium)
- "simple bodies" is their cute terminology for [atoms](chemistry.md#atom), whose existence at the time not yet full aknowledged [Section "History of the atomic theory"](chemistry.md#history-of-the-atomic-theory)
- we call it [polonium](chemistry.md#polonium)


> By carrying out these various operations, we obtain increasingly active products. Finally, we obtained a substance whose activity is about 400 times greater than that of [uranium](chemistry.md#uranium).
> 
> We have sought, among the bodies currently known, to see if there are any active ones. We have examined compounds of almost all [simple bodies](chemistry.md#atom); thanks to the great kindness of several chemists, we have had samples of the rarest substances. Uranium and thorium are the only ones clearly active, tantalum is perhaps very weakly so.
> 
> We therefore believe that the substance that we have removed from the [pitchblende](chemistry.md#uraninite) contains a metal not yet reported, close to bismuth by its analytical properties. If the existence of this new metal is confirmed, we propose to call it [polonium](chemistry.md#polonium), from the name of the country of origin of one of us.

They managed to purify it enough to look at the [emission spectrum](quantum-mechanics.md#emission-spectrum) and it seems novel:

> Mr. Demarçay was kind enough to examine the [spectrum of the body](quantum-mechanics.md#emission-spectrum) that we are studying. He was unable to distinguish any characteristic line apart from those due to impurities. This fact does not support the idea of ​​the existence of a new metal. However, Mr. Demarçay pointed out to us that uranium, thorium and tantalum offer particular spectra, formed of innumerable lines, very fine, difficult to perceive.

And once again, our [radiation](particle-physics.md#radiation)-based [analytical chemistry](chemistry.md#analytical-chemistry) technique is new:

> Allow us to note that if the existence of a new element is confirmed, this discovery will be uniquely attributable to the new method of detection that [Becquerel rays](#becquerel-s-rays) provide.

#### On a new, strongly radioactive substance contained in pitchblende

↑ **Parent:** [Publication by Marie Curie](#publication-by-marie-curie)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Comptes rendus de l'Académie des Sciences Tome 127](education.md#comptes-rendus-de-l-academie-des-sciences-tome-127)

This is the paper where [Marie Curie](#marie-curie) announced the discovery of [Radium](chemistry.md#radium).

It came out only 6 months after [the Polonium paper](#on-a-new-radioactive-substance-contained-in-pitchblende) and ended up in the same tome of the [Comptes rendus de l'Académie des Sciences](education.md#comptes-rendus-de-l-academie-des-sciences), [number 127](education.md#comptes-rendus-de-l-academie-des-sciences-tome-127) which is funny.

[French](linguistics.md#french-language) text on [Wikisource](law.md#wikisource): [https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Œuvres_de_Pierre_Curie/24](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Œuvres_de_Pierre_Curie/24). It's from a 1908 collection of works, and it has made minor corretions, such as using "[radioactive](particle-physics.md#radioactive-decay)" without dash instead of "radio-active" to update the terminology a bit, which is a crime!

The original can be found inside the original tome PDF: [https://archive.org/details/ComptesRendusAcademieDesSciences0127/page/n5/mode/2up](https://archive.org/details/ComptesRendusAcademieDesSciences0127/page/n5/mode/2up)

An [English](linguistics.md#english-language) translation by the [American Institute of Physics](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/american-institute-of-physics): [https://history.aip.org/exhibits/curie/discover.htm](https://history.aip.org/exhibits/curie/discover.htm)

Basically they extracted a [Barium](chemistry.md#barium) solution, but were unable to separate [Barium](chemistry.md#barium) and [Radium](chemistry.md#radium). If you look at a periodic table, you will see that [Radium](chemistry.md#radium) is directly below [radium](chemistry.md#radium) which explains it as they have very similar chemical properties.

> Two of us have shown that by purely chemical procedures it is possible to extract from [pitchblende](chemistry.md#uraninite) a strongly [radio-active](particle-physics.md#radioactive-decay) substance. This substance is related to [bismuth](chemistry.md#bismuth) by its analytical properties. We have expressed the opinion that perhaps the [pitchblende](chemistry.md#uraninite) contained a new element, for which we have proposed the name of polonium.
> 
> The investigations which we are following at present are in agreement with the first results we obtained, but in the course of these investigations we have come upon a second, strongly radio-active substance, entirely different from the first in its chemical properties. Specifically, polonium is precipitated from acid solution by hydrogen sulfide; its salts are soluble in acids and water precipitates them from solution; polonium is completely precipitated by ammonia.
> 
> The new radio-active substance which we have just found has all the chemical appearance of nearly pure barium: it is not precipitated either by hydrogen sulfide or by ammonium sulfide, nor by ammonia; its sulfate is insoluble in water and in acids; its carbonate is insoluble in water; its chloride, very soluble in water, is insoluble in concentrated hydrochloric acid and in alcohol. Finally this substance gives the easily recognized spectrum of barium.
> 
> We believe nevertheless that this substance, although constituted in its major part by barium, contains in addition a new element which gives it its radio-activity, and which, in addition, is closely related to barium in its chemical properties.

Like with [Polonium](chemistry.md#polonium) they once again found a new [spectral line](quantum-mechanics.md#spectral-line), though it was somewhat weak in this case as they didn't manage to purify as much:

> M. Demarçay has consented to examine the spectrum of our substance with a kindness which we cannot acknowledge too much. The results of his examinations are given in a special Note at the end of ours. Demarçay has found one line in the spectrum which does not seem due to any known element. This line, hardly visible with the chloride 60 times more active than uranium, has become prominent with the chloride enriched by fractionation to an activity 900 times that of uranium. The intensity of this line increases, then, at the same time as the radio-activity; that, we think, is a very serious reason for attributing it to the radio-active part of our substance.

Name the thing:

> The various reasons which we have enumerated lead us to believe that the new radio-active substance contains a new element to which we propose to give the name of [radium](chemistry.md#radium).

They did not have enough purity to clearly measure the mass difference:

> We have measured the atomic weight of our active barium, determining the chlorine in its anhydrous chloride. We have found numbers which differ very little from those obtained in parallel measurements on inactive barium chloride; the numbers for the active barium are always a little larger, but the difference is of the order of magnitude of the experimental errors.

but it is cute to see that they called radium as "active [barium](chemistry.md#barium)".

[Polonium](chemistry.md#polonium) and [radium](chemistry.md#radium) can be used as a [light source](photon.md#light-source) without [power source](technology.md#power-source), so oops, it looks like we broke the [conservation of energy](physics.md#conservation-of-energy)!

> The new radio-active substance certainly includes a very large portion of barium; in spite of that, the radio-activity is considerable. The radio-activity of radium then must be enormous.
> 
> Uranium, thorium, polonium, radium, and their compounds make the air a conductor of electricity and act photographically on sensitive plates. In these respects, polonium and radium are considerably more active than uranium and thorium. On photographic plates one obtains good impressions with radium and polonium in a half-minute's exposure; several hours are needed to obtain the same result with uranium and thorium.
> 
> The rays emitted by the components of polonium and radium make barium platinocyanide fluorescent; their action in this regard is analogous to that of the Röntgen rays, but considerably weaker. To perform the experiment, one lays over the active substance a very thin aluminum foil on which is spread a thin layer of barium platinocyanide; in the darkness the platinocyanide appears faintly luminous above the active substance.
> 
> In this manner a source of light is obtained, which is very feeble to tell the truth, but which operates without a source of energy. Here is at least an apparent contradiction to Carnot's Principle.
> 
> Uranium and thorium give no light under these conditions, their action being probably too weak.

Later on [radium](chemistry.md#radium) came to be used as a [phosphorescent](condensed-matter-physics.md#phosphorescence) light source for things like watch handles, which led to girls getting cancer in the factories; the [Radium Girls](chemistry.md#radium-girls).

[QED and the men who made it: Dyson, Feynman, Schwinger, and Tomonaga by Silvan Schweber (1994)](quantum-field-theory.md#qed-and-the-men-who-made-it-dyson-feynman-schwinger-and-tomonaga-by-silvan-schweber-1994) chapter 1.3 "P.A.M. Dirac and the Birth of Quantum Electrodynamics" quotes Dirac saying how being at high school during [World War I](science.md#world-war-i) was an advantage, since all slightly older boys were being sent to war, and so the younger kids were made advance as fast as they could through subjects. Exactly the type of thing [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) wants to achieve with [OurBigBook.com](ourbigbook-com.md), but without the need for a world war hopefully.

Dirac was a staunch [atheist](religion.md#atheism) having said during the [Fifth Solvay Conference (1927)](physics.md#fifth-solvay-conference-1927)[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Conference):

> If we are honest - and scientists have to be - we must admit that religion is a jumble of false assertions, with no basis in reality. The very idea of God is a product of the human imagination. It is quite understandable why primitive people, who were so much more exposed to the overpowering forces of nature than we are today, should have personified these forces in fear and trembling. But nowadays, when we understand so many natural processes, we have no need for such solutions. I can't for the life of me see how the postulate of an Almighty God helps us in any way. What I do see is that this assumption leads to such unproductive questions as why God allows so much misery and injustice, the exploitation of the poor by the rich and all the other horrors He might have prevented. If religion is still being taught, it is by no means because its ideas still convince us, but simply because some of us want to keep the lower classes quiet. Quiet people are much easier to govern than clamorous and dissatisfied ones. They are also much easier to exploit. Religion is a kind of opium that allows a nation to lull itself into wishful dreams and so forget the injustices that are being perpetrated against the people. Hence the close alliance between those two great political forces, the State and the Church. Both need the illusion that a kindly God rewards - in heaven if not on earth - all those who have not risen up against injustice, who have done their duty quietly and uncomplainingly. That is precisely why the honest assertion that God is a mere product of the human imagination is branded as the worst of all mortal sins.
