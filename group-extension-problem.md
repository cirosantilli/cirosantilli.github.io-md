# Group extension problem

↑ **Parent:** [Classification of finite groups](classification-of-finite-groups.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_extension)

Besides the understandable Wikipedia definition, [Video 1. "Simple Groups - Abstract Algebra by Socratica (2018)"](classification-of-finite-simple-groups.md#video-simple-groups-abstract-algebra-by-socratica-2018) gives an understandable one:

> Given a finite group $F$ and a simple group $S$, find all groups $G$ such that $N$ is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md) of $G$ and $G/N = S$.

We don't really know how to make up larger groups from smaller simple groups, which would complete the [classification of finite groups](classification-of-finite-groups.md):
- [https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/25315/how-is-a-group-made-up-of-simple-groups](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/25315/how-is-a-group-made-up-of-simple-groups)

In particular, this is hard because you can't just take the [direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) to retrieve the original group: [Section "Relationship between the quotient group and direct products"](relationship-between-the-quotient-group-and-direct-products.md).
