ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_name)

Middle names are a weird custom.

It's like a semi-secret part of your name that you only randomly use sometimes, and usually only in initials, and which no one else knows except your family.

It is as if the [Anglo-Saxons](united-kingdom.md#anglo-saxons) were preparing for a real world [Death Note](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/death-note) attack [I know your true name](literature.md#i-know-your-true-name)-style.

###### Western pseudonym of East Asian person

↑ **Parent:** [Pseudonym](#pseudonym)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [East Asia](continent.md#east-asia)

Many [East Asians](continent.md#east-asian), notably [Chinese](china.md) immigrants, choose to adopt a Western name [pseudonym](#pseudonym) to make it easier for Western people who don't speak the language to call them and remember their name.

Cowards! [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) would much rather just torture foreigners into learning his language. But fair play.

More interestingly however, some of the names chosen are not typical names, and some end up being very cute or mildly funny. Perhaps it is partly linked to [given names are getting weirder](#given-names-are-getting-weirder).

##### Abugida

ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abugida)

Somewhat midway between a [syllabary](#syllabary) and an [alphabet](#alphabet): you write out consonants, and vowels are "punctuation-like-modifiers".

E.g.: the main [Hindi](#hindi) script ([devanagari](#devanagari)) and most other [Indian](continent.md#india) languages.

###### Abjad

↑ **Parent:** [Abugida](#abugida)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad)

Unlike [abugida](#abugida), these actually make you guess vowels, which are mostly or all not written down in any way. Terrible.

E.g.: the main [Arabic](#arabic) script.

↑ **Parent:** [Alphabet](#alphabet)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet)

Unfortunately, [physicists](physicist.md) and [mathematicians](mathematics.md#mathematician) keep using Greek letters in their formulas, so we just have to learn them.

A helpful way to remember is to learn a bit of their history/pronunciation: [Section "Historical correspondence between Latin and Greek"](#historical-correspondence-between-latin-and-greek).

To learn the greek letters if you have a base latin alphabet, you _must_ learn the sound of each letter, and which [Latin letters](#latin-letter) they correspond to.

Symbols that look like Greek letters but are not Greek letters:
- [nabla symbol](calculus.md#nabla-symbol)

Is [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) crazy (he is, but for this point specifically), or do many/most [Greek letters](#greek-alphabet) represent the mouth position used in the pronunciation of the letter?
