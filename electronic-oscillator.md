# Electronic oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Electronic component](electronic-component.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_oscillator)

Something where [DC voltage](direct-current.md) comes in, and a periodic voltage comes out.

<a id="video-oscillators-rc-lc-crystal-by-greatscott-2015"></a>
**[Video 9](#video-oscillators-rc-lc-crystal-by-greatscott-2015). Oscillators: RC, LC, Crystal by GreatScott! (2015)** [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eYVOdlK15Og). Good video. Contains actual [breadboard](breadboard.md) experiments on [oscilloscope](oscilloscope.md) and circuit diagrams
- [https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=66](https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=66) [RC oscillator](rc-oscillator.md) on [breadboard](breadboard.md). Produces [rectangular wave](rectangular-wave.md). Mentions popular [integrated circuit](integrated-circuit.md) that does it: [555 timer IC](555-timer-ic.md).
- [https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=175](https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=175) [LC oscillators](lc-oscillator.md) allows for higher frequencies. Produces [sinusoidal](sinusoidal.md) output on [MHz](megahertz.md) range. Uses an amplifier to feed back into input and maintain same voltage. Hard to make reliably on breadboard.
- [https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=315](https://youtu.be/eYVOdlK15Og?t=315) [crystal oscillator](crystal-oscillator.md). Mentions it acts like an [LC oscillators](lc-oscillator.md). Shows and equivalent model. Wish he had talked more about them. You need support components around it: similarly to the LC case, the amplifier is generally not packaged in.
