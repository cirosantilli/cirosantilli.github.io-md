# University

↑ **Parent:** [Education](education.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University)

As of 2020, [university has the following very important applications](education.md#education-is-broken):
- meet an [intelligent sexual partner](ciro-santilli.md#ciro-santilli-s-wife) (see also: [Section "Sexual selection"](biology.md#sexual-selection), [Section "The main function of university is sexual selection"](#the-main-function-of-university-is-sexual-selection)) and or have fun and or [come out of the closet](biology.md#coming-out)
- get you in debt if you are from the [United States](united-states.md)
Notably, [Education is an IQ test](education.md#education-is-an-iq-test), not a way to learn [useful](how-to-teach.md#explain-how-to-make-money-with-the-lesson) and [beautiful](how-to-teach.md#explain-why-the-subject-is-beautiful) things.

One major issue is that teachers don't have the right incentive to, nor are selected to, teach well. Thus the existence of [Rate My Professors](website.md#rate-my-professors)! But we can do better...

Which is why [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) wants to destroy its current format with [OurBigBook.com](ourbigbook-com.md). He believes that we can find a more efficient organization to achieve both the social and research functions of university, by first doing as much as possible online

Ciro's university experiences are mentioned at: [Ciro Santilli's formal education](ciro-santilli.md#ciro-santilli-s-formal-education).

### University should focus on inspiring and not on evaluating

↑ **Parent:** [The ideal university](#the-ideal-university)

As of 2020s and much earlier, [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) believes that [undergrad](#undergraduate-education) studies were fundamentally broken (considering the [Information Age](science.md#information-age) which completely changed what would be possible) because university had only two goals, with the exception of a few enlightened professors:
- rank students from worse to best so they can get into [PhD](education.md#doctor-of-philosophy) programs.

  For regular jobs grades didn't even matter as much compared the prestige of your university (and therefore, [university entry exam](#university-entry-exam) grades) and your ability to stand the stress of exams to get minimal passing grade.

  In particular, being able to rank requires setting the difficulty level at a point where you can see a [normal distribution](calculus.md#normal-distribution) in grades, and not have everyone at either 0 nor 100%.

  Also, this split could be caused by either shitty learning materials/conditions, or by mere volume. It doesn't matter.
- get money from the students. Of course, in countries where university is "free", this means reporting how many students you had to some government office so they can give you a corresponding budget. But you still have an incentive to enroll as many as possible.
As a result, most students, who would not go on to do a [PhD](education.md#doctor-of-philosophy) essentially do a simple trade: all their time, and possibly some money, in exchange for imbuing themselves with the incredible name of a respected institution so they can get better jobs later on.

Beauty, deep understanding, and learning awesome things comes basically as a second thought.

### The only reason for universities to exist should be the laboratories

↑ **Parent:** [The ideal university](#the-ideal-university)

Besides of course [sexual selection](#the-main-function-of-university-is-sexual-selection), considering in this section only "formal learning" activities.

Consider e.g. the 2020 [University of Oxford](university-of-oxford.md), where many many people are taking courses without any [laboratory](science.md#laboratory) work (and also without much use at all) like literature and history, and they are paying about 9k [pounds](social-technology.md#pound-sterling)/year for it: [how much it costs to study at the University of Oxford?](university-of-oxford.md#university-of-oxford-study-costs).

Basically all of this could be done [online from books](ourbigbook-com.md).

Laboratories are impossible however, because expendables of every experiment you do cost from hundreds to thousands of dollars, not to mention crazy upfront equipment costs.

For this reason, the brick and mortar aspect universities should focus exclusively on laboratories, and ensuring that the students with the most relevant knowledge (which can be readily obtained online) get access to those laboratories. Students should of course fully master every aspect of theory pertinent to their experiments. [principal investigators](education.md#principal-investigator) should hand pick whichever criteria they want to select their students, possibly based partly on [exam as a service](education.md#exam-as-a-service) if they find it a useful metric.

Furthermore, the use of laboratories should put great focus on [novel research](education.md#novel-result). A lot of laboratory instruction could be done from video of an experiments. As much as possible, we should use laboratories for novel research. Related: [Section "Videos of all key physics experiments"](todo.md#videos-of-all-key-physics-experiments).

#### When in doubt, choose the course that has the most experimental work

↑ **Parent:** [The only reason for universities to exist should be the laboratories](#the-only-reason-for-universities-to-exist-should-be-the-laboratories)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Cirism](cirism.md)

You can always learn pure theory later on for free or very cheap from books.

And above all, you can always learn [software engineering](software.md#software-engineering) later on for free, because the programming community is so much more open than any other so far, notably e.g. with [Stack Overflow](stack-overflow.md) and [GitHub](software.md#github), see also: [Section "Ciro Santilli's Open Source Enlightenment"](ciro-santilli.md#ciro-santilli-s-open-source-enlightenment). [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) is trying to change that with [OurBigBook.com](ourbigbook-com.md), but don't hold your breath. But it is increasingly hard to understand [why there isn't an university that forces teachers to publish all their notes and lecture videos (which should be mandatorily recorded) with a Creative Commons License](#force-public-university-teachers-to-publish-their-teaching-material-with-an-open-license), and then let [anyone take whichever exams they want for a small fee or for free](education.md#exam-as-a-service).

Actually, there is a good chance you will learn to program, like it or not, because chances are that you won't be able to find as decent a job doing anything else.

But there is one thing you cannot learn for free: laboratory work. Laboratory work is just [too expensive to carry out outside of an institution](#the-only-reason-for-universities-to-exist-should-be-the-laboratories).

Basically, if you don't do laboratory work in [undergrad](#undergraduate-education), you will very likely never be able to do so in your entire life.

Because laboratories are so rare and expensive, it is laboratories that put you in the best most unfair position at creating world changing [deep tech](technology.md#deep-tech) startups, which is why [when in doubt, choose the course that has the most experimental work](#when-in-doubt-choose-the-course-that-has-the-most-experimental-work). Yes, you won't be able to achieve those insanely concentrated [equities](economy.md#equity-finance) of the early-[Internet](computer.md#internet), as you will need more [venture capital](company.md#venture-capital) to run your company, but those days are over now, deal with it.

<h5 id="molecular-sciences-course-of-the-university-of-sao-paulo">Molecular Sciences Course of the University of São Paulo</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Innovative university course](#innovative-university-course)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Good](cirism.md#good)

[http://www.cecm.usp.br/](http://www.cecm.usp.br/)

[https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curso_de_Ciências_Moleculares](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curso_de_Ciências_Moleculares)

Good [Portuguese](linguistics.md#portuguese-language) overview: [http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1806-11172017000300301&lng=pt&tlng=pt](http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1806-11172017000300301&lng=pt&tlng=pt)

A fantastic sounding full time 4-year course that any student could transfer to called that teaches various [natural science](science.md#natural-science) topics, notably [mathematics](mathematics.md), [physics](physics.md), [chemistry](chemistry.md) and [molecular biology](molecular-biology.md).

Many past students Ciro talked to however share a common frustration with the course: in the first 2 years at least, the "basic cycle", you have infinitely many courses, and no time to study, and [no choice of what to study](cirism.md#students-must-have-a-flexible-choice-of-what-to-learn), it is only in the latter 2 years (the advanced cycle) that you get the choices.

Also, if you get low grades in a single subject, your out. And [exams are useless](education.md#exam) of course.

Here's a [Quora](website.md#quora) question in [Portuguese](linguistics.md#portuguese-language) about the course: [https://pt.quora.com/Como-funciona-o-tal-do-curso-secreto-da-USP](https://pt.quora.com/Como-funciona-o-tal-do-curso-secreto-da-USP), the only decent answer so far being: [https://pt.quora.com/Como-funciona-o-tal-do-curso-secreto-da-USP/answer/Victor-Soares-31](https://pt.quora.com/Como-funciona-o-tal-do-curso-secreto-da-USP/answer/Victor-Soares-31). Very disappointing to hear.

On the advanced cycle, you have a lot of academic freedom. You are basically supposed to pick a research project with an advisor and go for it, with a small amount of mandatory course hours. Ciro was told in 2022 that you can even have advisors from other universities or industry, and that it is perfectly feasible to take courses in another university and validate the course hours later on. Fantastic!!!

Students from the entire [University of São Paulo](#university-of-sao-paulo) can apply to transfer to it only after joining the university, with the guarantee that they can go back to their original courses if they don't adapt to the new course, which is great!

Not doing it is one of [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md)'s regrets in life, see also: [don't be a pussy](don-t-be-a-pussy.md).

Around 2007, they were in a really shady building of the University, but when Ciro checked in 2021, they had apparently moved to a shiny new entrepreneurship-focused building. Fantastic news!!!

This place has one of the best changes of spawning the first [Brazilian](brazil.md) [Nobel Prize](nobel-prize.md) or [unicorn](company.md#unicorn-startup).

One of the [Brazilians](brazil.md) who came to [École Polytechnique](ecole-polytechnique.md) together with Ciro was from this course. The fact that he is one of the most intelligent people Ciro knows gave further credit to that course in his eyes.

<h5 id="sao-remo-the-favela-next-to-usp">São Remo, the favela next to USP</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [University of São Paulo](#university-of-sao-paulo)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Favela](brazil.md#favela)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Remo,_the_favela_next_to_USP)

[https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Remo](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Remo)

While in Brazil, [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) used to walk through the outskirts of a small [favela](brazil.md#favela) to get [to university](#university-of-sao-paulo) every day, the [Favela de São Remo](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Remo).

See the street view for: [R. Catumbi - Vila Butantã Vila Butantã, São Paulo - State of São Paulo, Brazil](https://goo.gl/maps/ZVnTjxNpsK6QPqTE9), e.g. [with this link](https://www.google.com/maps/@-23.5611635,-46.7449982,3a,75y,77.15h,72.78t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1swNEwJR0x5wkuKHvp7c0IHw!2e0!7i16384!8i8192).

To the left, the outer walls of a large police station, with [concertina wire](science.md#concertina-wire) on top and all.

To the right, dudes [selling drugs](biology.md#drug-traffic) on the entry of a small corridor street, presumably to which they could easily escape to in case of need.

The cops could have identified the dealers with binoculars if they actually wanted to!!!

The drug sellers did keep the peace in their business area, and Ciro never got robbed, and would come back from university parties on foot late through the favela.

But Ciro's friends did say that things got much worse after Ciro left, for example [a flash kidnapping was reported in 2015](http://g1.globo.com/sao-paulo/noticia/2015/01/engenheiro-e-vitima-de-sequestro-relampago-na-cidade-universitaria.html).

Wikipedia says that this favela started in the 60s and 70s as settlements of the builders of the University, and that many of the people there still work for the University.

This is consistent with the terribly old buildings Ciro saw when he was at university. They even had the building skills to build their own homes.

The state just has to either [legalize](law.md) those people, or give them houses somewhere else nearby. A [world class University is the most important thing a poor country can have](what-poor-countries-have-to-do-to-get-richer.md), and its image cannot be jeopardized like that.

The existence of that favela, right next to one of the most important universities in Latin America, puts [Brazil](brazil.md)'s surreal social inequality into perspective. Especially considering that before extremely heavy [university entry quotas](cirism.md#university-entry-quotas) were added, basically all students of the university (or at least of the courses that lead to high paying jobs) had attended private schools, and therefore were not of the poorer classes (see passage about 10 out 500 passage from [Section "Free gifted education"](cirism.md#free-gifted-education)).

The janitors of the apartment block Ciro lived all lived in the favela. Yes, in poor countries lives are worth nothing, and some poorer people work by watching the entrance of buildings of less poor people 24/7 to guard it from other even more desperate poor people who might want to rob the not so poor inhabitants. They also do janitor jobs like cleaning common areas in parallel.

They were incredibly nice hard-working people, and Ciro spoke often with them. If only given the opportunity, those people could be amazing engineers or scientists obviously. Ciro was also glad to be their friends, and sat down with them quite a few times for several minutes after coming back from University parties, partly because he felt bad about them having to work at that time, but also partly because he just liked them. And they were always up to date on who had come back with a girl to the apartment or not. Ciro imagines that if it had been him, it would have been a perfect bragging opportunity ;)

They had "nothing" but were still happy. This is true wisdom, and a good reminder [that all our non-transhumanist technical goals are nothing](religion.md#buddhism).

We must destroy [social inequality](economy.md#social-inequality).

#### Most British universities are registered as charities

↑ **Parent:** [British university](#british-university)

No, they are basically [not-for-profits](social-technology.md#nonprofit-organization), or more precisely in british legal terms, "[charities](social-technology.md#nonprofit-organization)". By taking government funding (directly or indirectly through subsiding enrolment fees?), they have to follow some government rules, and all major ones do it seems: [https://academia.stackexchange.com/questions/49187/in-what-sense-are-uk-universities-public/49188](https://academia.stackexchange.com/questions/49187/in-what-sense-are-uk-universities-public/49188)

A similar confusing naming pattern appears to apply to [Public school](united-kingdom.md#public-school-united-kingdom).

In the [University of Cambridge](#university-of-cambridge) for example, all MA degree holders or higher appear to have some voting power: [https://www.cam.ac.uk/about-the-university/how-the-university-and-colleges-work/governance](https://www.cam.ac.uk/about-the-university/how-the-university-and-colleges-work/governance) ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20210811192322/https://www.cam.ac.uk/about-the-university/how-the-university-and-colleges-work/governance))

This adds an extra layer of difficulty for the average taxpayer to make changes to university policy, e.g. making universities publish all material with [Creative Commons licenses](law.md#creative-commons-license). At most, voters could require this indirectly through the government funding requisites. It is a mess.

Not even the [Open University](#open-university) seems to be very open!

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) once attended a round table in the early 2020s where a [University of Oxford](university-of-oxford.md) official from the IP licensing department. The [University of Oxford](university-of-oxford.md) took a 20% equity on [spin-off companies](company.md#spin-off-company), not an uncommon [University IP ownership](#university-ip-ownership) policy at the time. At one point, the officer clearly justified this along the following very official sounding lines (paraphrased):

> The university is a [charity](social-technology.md#nonprofit-organization) with the goal of promoting education and research. All money obtained is reinvested in furthering education and research.

While noble sounding, this immediately reminded Ciro of [instrumental convergence](artificial-intelligence.md#instrumental-convergence), in the field of [AGI](artificial-intelligence.md#artificial-general-intelligence) philosophy. Or in other words, of course the best approach to maximize education and research outcomes of society is to first [take over the world](social-technology.md#world-domination), and then implement those goals from there! See also [Why Not Just: Think of AGI Like a Corporation? by Robert Miles (2018)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L5pUA3LsEaw)

Notably, the [University of Oxford](university-of-oxford.md) was extremely protective of its learning material at that time, which was highly paywalled behind university logins, presumably with the rationale of having unique learning materials to enroll more paying undergrads. How can giving out free information to all not be the optimal way to "promoting education and research" is very hard to envision.

##### Open University

↑ **Parent:** [List of British universities](#list-of-british-universities)

Not really dedicated to open source course material, nor to free courses...

The "Open" in its name only made sense in the 60's, when it was founded, nowadays, there isn't much about this institution that is very different compared to traditional [Oxbridge](university-of-oxford.md#oxbridge). "Cheap more online university" would be a more adequate name for it.

A system that would truly live up to the name "Open" in the year 2020 is the one described at [the ideal university](#the-ideal-university) by [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md).

Wikipedia even says that the initial focus was on broadcasting learning material on [television](telecommunication.md#television) and [radio](telecommunication.md#radio), so what happened to that now that we have an even more powerful on-demand tool called [Internet](computer.md#internet)!

They even created their own MOOC website, [FutureLearn](website.md#futurelearn). But [https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/massive-open-online-courses-started-out-completely-free-but-where-are-they-now-1dd1020f59/](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/massive-open-online-courses-started-out-completely-free-but-where-are-they-now-1dd1020f59/) mentions:

> The course content is still free to access, but it’s only available for the duration of the course, and for two weeks after it ends.

OMG. God why.

> The OU provides around 5% of its formal course materials as free open educational content every year on OpenLearn
