# Sequence alignment

↑ **Parent:** [Bioinformatics](bioinformatics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_alignment)

Sequence alignment is trying to match a [DNA](dna-split.md) or [amino acid](amino-acid.md) sequence, even though the sequences might not be exactly the same, otherwise it would be a straight up [string-search algorithm](string-search-algorithm.md).

This is fundamental in [bioinformatics](bioinformatics.md) for two reasons:
- when you [sequence the DNA](dna-sequencing.md) of a new [species](species.md), you can guess what each [protein](protein-split.md) does by comparing it with similar proteins in other species that you have already studied
- when doing [DNA sequencing](dna-sequencing.md), and specially [short-read DNA sequencing](short-read-dna-sequencing.md), you generally need to align the reads to reference genomes to know where you are inside the entire genome, and then be able to spot [mutations](mutation.md), notably [single-nucleotide polymorphisms](single-nucleotide-polymorphism.md)
