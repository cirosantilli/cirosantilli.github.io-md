<h1 id="noether-s-theorem">Noether's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrangian mechanics](lagrangian-mechanics.md)

For every [continuous symmetry](continuous-symmetry.md) in the system ([Lie group](lie-group.md)), there is a corresponding conservation law.

Furthermore, given the symmetry, we can calculate the derived conservation law, and vice versa.

As mentioned at [http://buzzard.ups.edu/courses/2017spring/projects/schumann-lie-group-ups-434-2017.pdf](http://buzzard.ups.edu/courses/2017spring/projects/schumann-lie-group-ups-434-2017.pdf), what the symmetry ([Lie group](lie-group.md)) acts on (obviously?!) are the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md) [generalized coordinates](generalized-coordinate.md). And from that, we immediately guess that manifolds are going to be important, because the generalized variables of the Lagrangian can trivially be [Non-Euclidean geometry](non-euclidean-geometry.md), e.g. the [pendulum](pendulum.md) lives on an infinite cylinder.
