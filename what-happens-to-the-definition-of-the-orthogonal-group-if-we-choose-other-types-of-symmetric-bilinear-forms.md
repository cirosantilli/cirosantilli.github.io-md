# What happens to the definition of the orthogonal group if we choose other types of symmetric bilinear forms

↑ **Parent:** [The orthogonal group is the group of all matrices that preserve the dot product](the-orthogonal-group-is-the-group-of-all-matrices-that-preserve-the-dot-product.md)

We looking at the definition [the orthogonal group is the group of all matrices that preserve the dot product](the-orthogonal-group-is-the-group-of-all-matrices-that-preserve-the-dot-product.md), we notice that the [dot product](dot-product.md) is one example of [positive definite symmetric bilinear form](positive-definite-symmetric-bilinear-form.md), which in turn can also be represented by a matrix as shown at: [Section "Matrix representation of a symmetric bilinear form"](matrix-representation-of-a-symmetric-bilinear-form.md).

By looking at this more general point of view, we could ask ourselves what happens to the group if instead of the [dot product](dot-product.md) we took a more general [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md), e.g.:
- $I_2$: another [positive definite symmetric bilinear form](positive-definite-symmetric-bilinear-form.md) such as $(x_1, x_2)^T(y_1, y_2) = 2 x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2$?
- $I_-$ what if we drop the [positive definite](positive-definite-matrix.md) requirement, e.g. $(x_1, x_2)^T(y_1, y_2) = - x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2$?
The answers to those questions are given by the [Sylvester's law of inertia](sylvester-s-law-of-inertia.md) at [Section "All indefinite orthogonal groups of matrices of equal metric signature are isomorphic"](all-indefinite-orthogonal-groups-of-matrices-of-equal-metric-signature-are-isomorphic.md).
