### Basal (phylogenetics)

↑ **Parent:** [Phylogenetics](#phylogenetics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_(phylogenetics))

When a characteristic is basal, it basically means the opposite of it being [polyphyletic](#polyphyly).

E.g. [monotremes](#monotreme) laying eggs did not evolve separately after function loss, it comes directly from [reptiles](#reptile).

###### Why it takes several days to enter inflammatory phase in COVID-19?

↑ **Parent:** [COVID-19](#covid-19)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_it_takes_several_days_to_enter_inflammatory_phase_in_COVID-19?)

[https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7247969/](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7247969/)

Why is it there such a clear separation of phases?

Why do people with mild symptoms go on to die? It is a great mystery.

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md)'s theory is that COVID is extremely effective at avoiding [immune response](biology.md#immune-response). Then, in people where this is effective, things reach a point where there is so much virus, that the body notices and moves on to take a more drastic approach. This is compatible with the virus killing older people more, as they have weaker immunes systems. This is however incompatible with the fact that people don't seem to be contagious after the viral phase is over...

###### E. Coli replication time

↑ **Parent:** [Escherichia coli](#escherichia-coli)

20 minutes in optimal conditions, with a crazy multiple start sites mechanism: [E. Coli starts DNA replication before the previous one finished](#e-coli-starts-dna-replication-before-the-previous-one-finished).

Otherwise, naively, would take 60-90 minutes just to replicate and segregate the full DNA otherwise. So it starts copying multiple times.

- "L-homoserine" into "L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde"
- "L-aspartate" into "4-phospho-L-aspartate"
Also interestingly, we see that both of those reaction require some extra energy to catalyse, one needing [adenosine triphosphate](cell.md#adenosine-triphosphate) and the other [nADP+](protein.md#nadp-plus).

- £355.00 in 2019

Size: 300 x 600 nm

Reproduction time: [https://www.quora.com/unanswered/How-long-do-Mycoplasma-bacteria-take-to-reproduce-under-optimal-conditions](https://www.quora.com/unanswered/How-long-do-Mycoplasma-bacteria-take-to-reproduce-under-optimal-conditions)

Has one of the smallest genomes known, and [JCVI](research-institute.md#j-craig-venter-institute) made a minimized strain with 473 genes: [JCVI-syn3.0](#jcvi-syn3-0).

The reason why genitalium has such a small genome is that [parasites tend to have smaller DNAs](dna.md#parasites-tend-to-have-smaller-dnas). So it must be highlighted that genitalium can only survive in highly enriched environments, it can't even make its own [amino acids](protein.md#amino-acid), which it normally obtains fromthe host cells! And because it cannot do [cellular respiration](cell.md#cellular-respiration), it very likely replicates slower than say [E. Coli](#escherichia-coli). It's easy to be small in such scenarios!

[Power, Sex, Suicide by Nick Lane (2006)](cell.md#power-sex-suicide-by-nick-lane-2006) section "How to lose the cell wall without dying" page 184 has some related mentions puts it well very:

> One group, the Mycoplasma, comprises mostly parasites, many of which live inside other cells. Mycoplasma cells are tiny, with very small genomes. [M. genitalium](#mycoplasma-genitalium), discovered in 1981, has the smallest known genome of any bacterial cell, encoding fewer than  genes. Despite its simplicity, it ranks among the most common of sexually transmitted diseases, producing symptoms similar to Chlamydia infection. It is so small (less than a third of a micron in diameter, or an order of magnitude smaller than most bacteria) that it must normally be viewed under the [electron microscope](microscopy.md#electron-microscope); and difﬁculties culturing it meant its signiﬁcance was not appreciated until the important advances in gene sequencing in the early 1990s. Like Rickettsia, Mycoplasma have lost virtually all the genes required for making [nucleotides](dna.md#nucleotide), [amino acids](protein.md#amino-acid), and so forth. Unlike Rickettsia, however, Mycoplasma have also lost all the genes for oxygen respiration, or indeed any other form of membrane respiration: they have no cytochromes, and so must rely on [fermentation](cell.md#fermentation) for energy.

###### Monotreme

↑ **Parent:** [Mammal subclade](#mammal-subclade)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotreme)

The weirdest [mammal](#mammal) [clade](#clade): they lay fucking [eggs](biology.md#egg). Only 5 known species alive as of 2020.

Eggs are [basal](#basal-phylogenetics): they simply didn't evolve out of what other [reptiles](#reptile) do. From which we conclude that [milk](#milk) came before [eggs](biology.md#egg) stopped.

So this is the most basal subclade of mammals.

Etymology: means "single hole" in [Greek](linguistics.md#greek-language), because like other [reptiles](#reptile) it has a single hole for [shit](molecular-biology.md#feces), [pee](molecular-biology.md#urine) and [fucking](biology.md#sexual-intercourse): the [cloaca](#cloaca).
