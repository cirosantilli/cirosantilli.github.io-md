# Definition of the indefinite orthogonal group

↑ **Parent:** [Indefinite orthogonal group](indefinite-orthogonal-group.md)

Given a [matrix](matrix.md) $A$ with [metric signature](metric-signature.md) containing $m$ positive and $n$ negative entries, the [indefinite orthogonal group](indefinite-orthogonal-group.md) is the set of all matrices that preserve the [associated bilinear form](matrix-representation-of-a-bilinear-form.md), i.e.:

$$
O(m, n) = {O \in M(m + n) | \forall x, y x^T A y = (Ox)^T A (Oy)}
$$

Note that if $A = I$, we just have the standard [dot product](dot-product.md), and that subcase corresponds to the following definition of the [orthogonal group](orthogonal-group.md): [Section "The orthogonal group is the group of all matrices that preserve the dot product"](the-orthogonal-group-is-the-group-of-all-matrices-that-preserve-the-dot-product.md).

As shown at [all indefinite orthogonal groups of matrices of equal metric signature are isomorphic](all-indefinite-orthogonal-groups-of-matrices-of-equal-metric-signature-are-isomorphic.md), due to the [Sylvester's law of inertia](sylvester-s-law-of-inertia.md), only the metric signature of $A$ matters. E.g., if we take two different matrices with the same metric signature such as:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 0
0 -1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

and:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
2 0
0 -3
\end{bmatrix}
$$

both produce [isomorphic](isomorphism.md) spaces. So it is customary to just always pick the matrix with only +1 and -1 as entries.
