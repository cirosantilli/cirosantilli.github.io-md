# Galilean invariance

↑ **Parent:** [Galilean transformation](galilean-transformation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_invariance)

A [law of physics](law-of-physics.md) is Galilean invariant if the same formula works both when you are standing still on land, or when you are on a boat moving at constant velocity.

For example, if we were describing the movement of a [point particle](point-particle.md), the exact same formulas that predict the evolution of $x_{land}(t)$ must also predict $x_{boat}(t)$, even though of course both of those $x(t)$ will have different values. 

It would be extremely unsatisfactory if the formulas of the [laws of physics](law-of-physics.md) did not obey [Galilean invariance](galilean-invariance.md). Especially if you remember that [Earth](earth.md) is travelling extremelly fast relative to the [Sun](sun.md). If there was no such invariance, that would mean for example that the [laws of physics](law-of-physics.md) would be different in other [planets](planet.md) that are moving at different speeds. That would be a strong sign that our laws of physics are not complete.

The consequence/cause of that is that you cannot know if you are moving at a constant speed or not.

[Lorentz invariance](lorentz-invariant.md) generalizes [Galilean invariance](galilean-invariance.md) to also account for [special relativity](special-relativity.md), in which a more complicated invariant that also takes into account different times observed in different [inertial frames of reference](inertial-frame-of-reference.md) is also taken into account. But the fundamental desire for the [Lorentz invariance](lorentz-invariant.md) of the [laws of physics](law-of-physics.md) remains the same.
