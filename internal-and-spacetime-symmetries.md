# Internal and spacetime symmetries

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum field theory](quantum-field-theory-split.md)

[https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/106392/internal-and-spacetime-symmetries](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/106392/internal-and-spacetime-symmetries)

The different only shows up for [field](field-physics.md), not with particles. For fields, there are two types of changes that we can make that can keep the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md) unchanged as mentioned at [Physics from Symmetry by Jakob Schwichtenberg (2015)](physics-from-symmetry-by-jakob-schwichtenberg-2015.md) chapter "4.5.2 Noether's Theorem for Field Theories - Spacetime":
- [spacetime symmetry](spacetime-symmetry.md): act with the [Poincaré group](poincare-group.md) on the [Four-vector](four-vector.md) spacetime inputs of the field itself, i.e. transforming $L(\Phi(x), \partial \Phi(x), dx)$ into $L(\Phi'(x'), \partial \Phi'(x'), x')$
- [internal symmetry](internal-symmetry.md): act on the output of the  field, i.e.: $L(\Phi(x) + \delta \Phi(x), \partial (\Phi(x) + \delta \Phi(x)), x)$

From [defining properties of elementary particles](defining-properties-of-elementary-particles.md):
- spacetime:
  - [mass](mass.md)
  - [spin](spin-physics.md)
- internal
  - [electric charge](electric-charge.md)
  - [Weak charge](weak-charge.md)
  - [color charge](color-charge.md)

From the spacetime theory alone, we can derive the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md) for the free theories for each [spin](spin-physics.md):
- [spin 0](spin-0.md): [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md)
- [spin half](spin-half.md): [Dirac equation](dirac-equation.md)
- [spin 1](spin-1.md): [Proca equation](proca-equation.md)
Then the internal symmetries are what add the interaction part of the [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md), which then completes the [Standard Model Lagrangian](standard-model-lagrangian.md).
