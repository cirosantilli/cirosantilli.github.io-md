# Analog computer

↑ **Parent:** [Analog and digital computers](analog-and-digital-computers.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computer)

Some of the earlier computers of the 20th centure were analog computers, not digital.

At some point analog died however, and "computer" basically by default started meaning just "[digital computer](digital-computer.md)".

As of the 2010's and forward, with the limit of [Moore's law](moore-s-law.md) and the rise of [machine learning](machine-learning-split.md), people have started looking again into analog computing as a possile way forward. A key insight is that huge floating point precision is not that crucial in many [deep learning](deep-learning.md) applications, e.g. many new digital designs have tried [16-bit floating point](half-precision-floating-point-format.md) as opposed to the more traditional 32-bit minium. Some papers are even looking into 8-bit: [https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3327757.3327866](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3327757.3327866)

As an example, the [Lightmatter](lightmatter.md) company was trying to implement [silicon photonics](silicon-photonics.md)-based matrix multiplication.

A general intuition behind this type of development is that the [human brain](human-brain.md), the holy grail of [machine learning](machine-learning-split.md), is itself an [analog computer](analog-computer.md).
