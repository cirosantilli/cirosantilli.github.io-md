# Transversal time dilation

↑ **Parent:** [Time dilation](time-dilation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_dilation#Simple_inference_of_velocity_time_dilation)

Light watch transverse to direction of motion. This case is interesting because it separates [length contraction](length-contraction.md) from [time dilation](time-dilation.md) completely.

Of course, as usual in [special relativity](special-relativity.md), calling something "time dilation" leads us to [mind boggling](mind-blown.md) ideas of "symmetry breaking": if both frames have a light watch, how can both possibly observe the other to be time dilated?

And the answer to this, is the usual: in special relativity time and space are interwoven in a [fucked](sexual-intercourse.md) up way, everything is just a spacetime event.

In this case, there are three spacetime events of interest: both clocks start at same position, your beam hits up at x=0, moving frame hits up at x\>0.

Those two mentioned events are [spacelike-separated events](spacelike-separated-event.md), and therefore even though they seem simultaneous to you, they are not going to be simultaneous to the moving observer!

If [little clock one meter away from you](einstein-synchronization.md) tells you that at the time of some event (your light beam hit up) the moving light watch was only 50% up, this is just a number given by your one meter away watch!
