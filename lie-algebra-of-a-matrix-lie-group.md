# Lie algebra of a matrix Lie group

↑ **Parent:** [Matrix Lie group](matrix-lie-group.md)

For this sub-case, we can define the [Lie algebra](lie-algebra.md) of a Lie group $G$ as the set of all matrices $M \in G$ such that for all $t \in \R$:

$$
e^{tM} \in G
$$

If we fix a given $M$ and vary $t$, we obtain a [subgroup](subgroup.md) of $G$. This type of subgroup is known as a [one parameter subgroup](one-parameter-subgroup.md).

The immediate question is then if every element of $G$ can be reached in a unique way (i.e. is the exponential map a [bijection](bijection.md)). By looking at the [matrix logarithm](logarithm-of-a-matrix.md) however we conclude that this is not the case for [real](real-number.md) matrices, but it is for [complex](complex-number.md) matrices.

Examples:
- [Lie algebra of $GL(n)$](lie-algebra-of-gl-n.md)
- [Lie algebra of $SL(2)$](lie-algebra-of-sl-2.md)
- [Lie algebra of $SO(3)$](lie-algebra-of-so-3.md)
- [Lie algebra of $SU(2)$](lie-algebra-of-su-2.md)
