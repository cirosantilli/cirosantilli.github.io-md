# Alpha decay

↑ **Parent:** [Type of radioactive decay](type-of-radioactive-decay.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_decay)

Most of the [helium](helium.md) in the [Earth](earth.md)'s atmosphere comes from alpha decay, since helium is lighter than air and naturally escapes out out of the atmosphere.

Wiki mentions that alpha decay is well modelled as a [quantum tunnelling](quantum-tunnelling.md) event, see also [Geiger-Nuttall law](geiger-nuttall-law.md).

As a result of that law, alpha particles have relatively little energy variation around 5 MeV or a speed of about 5% of the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) for any element, because the energy is inversely exponentially proportional to [half-life](half-life.md). This is because:
- if the energy is much larger, decay is very fast and we don't have time to study the [isotope](isotope.md)
- if the energy is much smaller, decay is very rare and we don't have enough events to observe at all
