The only downside is the usage of slightly [supernatural martial arts](supernatural-martial-arts.md).

It must be said that the series gets worse as it progresses. The best part of it is the backstory of [individual characters](water-margin-character.md), and how noble men are made fight their government. As things progress however, it becomes more of a mass battles thing, so less interesting. This is perhaps a problem of the novel itself, which an adaption cannot avoid. The good part basically ends with the end of Wu Song's story at episode 22.

One of the most amazing things about this series is learning about how people lived in the past. It feels very realistic. Some amazing details:
- on the pharmacy, they used a leg powered grinder. [Ciro Santilli's wife](ciro-santilli-s-wife.md) said she had actually seen one those in use in China
- to indicate that something is for sale, the would attach some straws around it
- it is totally fine to talk to an old person about giving them money to buy a nice coffin
- some dudes drink [baijiu](baijiu.md) from bowls. One wonders how much truth there can be in that.

The [Big Dipper](big-dipper.md) is reference is made in the novel itself as part of a dream one of the leaders of the rebellion has. The dream is a premonition of the number of men that will gather to plot to steal expensive birthday gifts corrupt officials are sending one another.

The song perfectly captures the camaraderie shown in the novel, the immediacy with which sworn brothers follow one another into the riskiest of tasks, and the fight against injustice, which is the central theme.

To get an idea of its popularity, [Ciro Santilli's mother-in-law](ciro-santilli-s-mother-in-law.md) is able to complete sentences of the song by herself in her late 60's, and she isn't exactly a scholar!
