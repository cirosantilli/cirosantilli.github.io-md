<h1 id="maxwell-s-equations-require-special-relativity">Maxwell's equations require special relativity</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell's_equations_require_special_relativity)

The following aspects of [Maxwell's equations](maxwell-s-equations.md) make no sense without [special relativity](special-relativity.md):
- the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) would be different observers have different speeds, see e.g.: [charged particle moving at the same speed of electrons thought experiment](moving-magnet-and-conductor-problem.md)
- [Maxwell's equations imply that the speed of light is the same for all inertial reference frames](maxwell-s-equations-imply-that-the-speed-of-light-is-the-same-for-all-inertial-reference-frames.md)

When charged particle though experiment are seen from the point of view of special relativity, it becomes clear that magnetism is just a direct side effect of charges being viewed in special relativity. One is philosophically reminded of how [spin is the consequence of quantum mechanics + special relativity](the-dirac-equation-predicts-spin.md).
