# Explain how to make money with the lesson

↑ **Parent:** [How to teach](../how-to-teach-split.md)

<a id="_97"></a>
People will be more interested if they see how the stuff they are learning is useful.

<a id="_98"></a>
Useful 99% of the time means you can make [money](../money.md) with it.

<a id="_99"></a>
Achieving [novel results](../novel-result.md) for [science](../science-split.md), or charitable goals (e.g. creating novel tutorials) are also equaly valid. Note that those also imply you being able to make a living out of something, just that you will be getting donations and not become infinitey rich. and that is fine.

<a id="_100"></a>
Projects don't need of course to reach the level of [novel result](../novel-result.md). But they must at least aim at moving towards that.

<a id="_101"></a>
This is one of the greatest challenges of education, since a huge part of the useful information is locked under [enterprise or military secrecy](../military-industrial-complex.md), or even [open academic incomprehensibility](../the-missing-link-between-basic-and-advanced.md), making it nearly to impossible for the front-line educators to actually find and teach real use cases.
