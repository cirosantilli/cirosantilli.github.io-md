# Help students achieve their goal

↑ **Parent:** [How to teach](../how-to-teach-split.md)

<a id="_6"></a>
This is the [most important of all points](../students-must-have-a-flexible-choice-of-what-to-learn.md).

<a id="_7"></a>
Don't set goals for your students.

<a id="_8"></a>
Ask students what they want to do, and help them achieve that goal.

<a id="_9"></a>
If they don't know what to do, give suggestions of interesting things they could do.

<a id="_10"></a>
Once they have a goal, just help them learn everything that is needed to achieve that goal

<a id="_11"></a>
If they don't have a goal, any attempt to learn is a total and complete waste of time.

<a id="_12"></a>
This is because the universe of potentially useful things that can be learnt is infinite, and no human can ever learn everything.

<a id="_13"></a>
The only solution, is to try and learn only what seems necessary to reach your goal, and just try to reach your goal instead.

<a id="_14"></a>
This approach is called [backward design](../backward-design.md).

<a id="_15"></a>
Also, setting overly ambitious goals, is a good idea: [the side effects of ambitious goals are often the most valuable thing achieved](../the-side-effects-of-ambitious-goals-are-often-the-most-valuable-thing-achieved.md).

<a id="_16"></a>
"Graduating" and "getting a diploma" are not valid goals, because they are useless. A goal has to be either an amazing specific [technological](../deep-tech.md) or [artistic](../art-split.md) development.
