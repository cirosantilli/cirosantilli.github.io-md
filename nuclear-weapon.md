#### Gun-type fission weapon

↑ **Parent:** [Fission weapon by geometry](#fission-weapon-by-geometry)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun-type_fission_weapon)

[Gun-type fission weapons](#gun-type-fission-weapon) are the simplest approach and they work with [Uranium-235](chemistry.md#uranium-235) bombs as you can ignite it with just one explosion.

But [Gun-type fission weapons don't work with plutonium](#gun-type-fission-weapons-don-t-work-with-plutonium), and [weapon grade Plutonium is cheaper than weapon grade Uranium](chemistry.md#weapon-grade-plutonium-is-cheaper-than-weapon-grade-uranium), so it wasn't much used.

<h5 id="gun-type-fission-weapons-don-t-work-with-plutonium">Gun-type fission weapons don't work with plutonium</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Gun-type fission weapon](#gun-type-fission-weapon)

[Gun-type fission weapons](#gun-type-fission-weapon) don't work with [Plutonium-239](chemistry.md#plutonium-239) because of the presence of [Plutonium-240](chemistry.md#plutonium-240) as an impurity which leads to [fizzle](#fizzle-nuclear-explosion).

Good mentions at: [https://youtu.be/dgBDvnqMkT4?t=252](https://youtu.be/dgBDvnqMkT4?t=252)

#### Implosion-type fission weapon

↑ **Parent:** [Fission weapon by geometry](#fission-weapon-by-geometry)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon_design#Implosion-type)

[Implosion-type fission weapons](#implosion-type-fission-weapon) are more complicated than [gun-type fission weapon](#gun-type-fission-weapon) because you have to precisely coordinate the detonation of a bunch of explosives.

## Weapons-grade nuclear material

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear weapon](nuclear-weapon.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons-grade_nuclear_material)

For [nuclear weapons](nuclear-weapon.md) you need a certain level of [isotope](particle-physics.md#isotope) purity of either [plutonium-239](chemistry.md#plutonium-239) or [uranium-235](chemistry.md#uranium-235).

And the easiest way by far to achieve this purity is to produce [plutonium-239](chemistry.md#plutonium-239) in a [breeder reactor](particle-physics.md#breeder-reactor), which allows you to get it out with much cheaper chemical processes rather than costly [isotope separation](particle-physics.md#isotope-separation) methods.

[https://fissilematerials.org/](https://fissilematerials.org/) summarizes stockpiles and production status. [20224 Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20240815234449/https://fissilematerials.org/).

[https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/doe/pu50yc.html](https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/doe/pu50yc.html) mentions:

> The United States Government has used 14 [plutonium](chemistry.md#plutonium) production reactors at the [Hanford](#hanford-site) and [Savannah River](#savannah-river-site) sites to produce plutonium for the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile and DOE research and development programs. That site also contains a good summary of the closed shutdown reactors in each site. These are publicly disclosed e.g. at: [https://www.hanford.gov/page.cfm/ProjectsFacilities](https://www.hanford.gov/page.cfm/ProjectsFacilities)

##### Hanford site

↑ **Parent:** [American nuclear weapon facility](#american-nuclear-weapon-facility)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [American plutonium production](#american-plutonium-production)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanford_site)

The [B Reactor](#b-reactor) of the facility produced the [plutonium](chemistry.md#plutonium) used for [Trinity](#trinity-nuclear-test) and [Fat Man](#fat-man), and then for many more thousand bombs during the [Cold War](science.md#cold-war). More precisely, this was done at 

Located in [Washington](united-states.md#washington-state), in a dry place the middle of the mountainous areas of the Western [United States](united-states.md), where basically no one lives. The Columbia river is however nearby, that river is quite large, and provided the water needed by their activities, notably for cooling the [nuclear reactors](particle-physics.md#nuclear-reactor). It is worth it having look on [Google Maps](software.md#google-maps) to get a feel for the region.

Unlike many other such laboratories, this one did not become a [United States Department of Energy national laboratories](research-institute.md#united-states-department-of-energy-national-laboratory). It was likely just too polluted.

###### Metallurgical Laboratory

↑ **Parent:** [Chicago Pile-1](#chicago-pile-1)
