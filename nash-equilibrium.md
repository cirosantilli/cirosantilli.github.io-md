# Nash equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Game theory](game-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_equilibrium)

The best example to look at first is the [penalty kick left right Nash equilibrium](penalty-kick-left-right-nash-equilibrium.md).

Then, a much more interesting example is choosing a deck of a TCG competition: [Magic: The Gathering meta-based deck choice is a bimatrix game](magic-the-gathering-meta-based-deck-choice-is-a-bimatrix-game.md), which is the exact same, but each player has N choices rather than 2.

The next case that should be analyzed is the [prisoner's dilemma](prisoner-s-dilemma.md).

The key idea is that:
- imagine that the game will be played many times between two players
- if one player always chooses one deck, the other player will adapt by choosing the anti-deck
- therefore, the best strategy for both players, is to pick decks randomly, each with a certain probability. This type of probabilistic approach is called a [mixed strategy](mixed-strategy.md)
- if any player deviates from the equilibrium probability, then the other player can add more of the anti-deck to the deck that the other player deviated, and gain an edge

  Therefore, using equilibrium probabilities is the optimal way to play
