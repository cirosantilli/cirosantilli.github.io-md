# Mathieu group

↑ **Parent:** [Sporadic group](sporadic-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_group)

Contains the first [sporadic groups](sporadic-group.md) discovered by far: 11 and 12 in 1861, and 22, 23 and 24 in 1973. And therefore presumably the simplest! The next sporadic ones discovered were the [Janko groups](janko-group.md), only in 1965!

Each [$M_n$](matrix-ring.md) is a [permutation group](permutation-group.md) on $n$ elements. There isn't an obvious algorithmic relationship between $n$ and the actual group.

Their; [k-transitive group](k-transitive-group.md) properties seem to be the main characterization, according to Wikipedia:
- 22 is 3-transitive but not 4-transitive.
- four of them (11, 12, 23 and 24) are the only [sporadic](sporadic-group.md) [4-transitive](k-transitive-group.md) groups as per the [classification of 4-transitive groups](classification-of-4-transitive-groups.md) (no known simpler proof as of 2021), which sounds like a reasonable characterization. Note that 12 and 25 are also 5 transitive.
Looking at the [classification of k-transitive groups](classification-of-k-transitive-groups.md) we see that the Mathieu groups are the only families of 4 and 5 transitive groups other than [symmetric groups](symmetric-group.md) and [alternating groups](alternating-group.md). 3-transitive is not as nice, so let's just say it is the [stabilizer](stabilizer-group.md) of $M_{23}$ and be done with it.
