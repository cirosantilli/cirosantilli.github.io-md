# Einstein notation for partial derivatives

↑ **Parent:** [Einstein notation](einstein-notation.md)

The [Einstein summation convention](einstein-notation.md) works will with [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) and it is widely used in [particle physics](particle-physics-split.md).

In particular, the [divergence](divergence.md) and the [Laplacian](laplace-operator.md) can be succinctly expressed in this notation:
- [Section "Divergence in Einstein notation"](divergence-in-einstein-notation.md)
- [Section "Laplacian in Einstein notation"](laplacian-in-einstein-notation.md)

In order to express partial derivatives, we must use what [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli-split.md) calls the "[partial index partial derivative notation](partial-index-partial-derivative-notation.md)", which refers to variables with indices such as $x_0$, $x_1$, $x_2$, $\partial_0$, $\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$  instead of the usual letters $x$, $y$ and $z$.
