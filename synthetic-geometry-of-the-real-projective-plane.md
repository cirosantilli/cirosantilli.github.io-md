# Synthetic geometry of the real projective plane

↑ **Parent:** [Real projective plane](real-projective-plane.md)

It good to think about how [Euclid's postulates](euclid-s-postulates.md) look like in the real projective plane:
- two parallel lines on the plane meet at a point on the sphere!

  Since there is one point of infinity for each direction, there is one such point for every direction the two parallel lines might be at. The [parallel postulate](parallel-postulate.md) does not hold, and is replaced with a simpler more elegant version: every two lines meet at exactly one point.

  One thing to note however is that ther [real projective plane](real-projective-plane.md) does not have [angles](angle.md) defined on it by definition. Those can be defined, forming [elliptic geometry](elliptic-geometry.md) through the [projective model of elliptic geometry](projective-elliptic-geometry.md), but we can interpret the "parallel lines" as "two lines that meet at a point at infinity"
- points in the real projective plane are lines in [$\R^3$](real-coordinate-space-of-dimension-three.md)
- lines in the real projective plane are planes in [$\R^3$](real-coordinate-space-of-dimension-three.md).

  For every two projective points there is a single projective line that passes through them.

  Since it is a plane in [$\R^3$](real-coordinate-space-of-dimension-three.md), it always intersects the real plane at a line.

  Note however that not all lines in the real plane correspond to a projective line: only lines tangent to a circle at zero do.

Unlike the [real projective line](real-projective-line.md) which is [homotopic](homotopy.md) to the [circle](circle.md), the [real projective plane](real-projective-plane.md) is not [homotopic](homotopy.md) to the [sphere](sphere.md).

The [topological](topology.md) difference bewteen the [sphere](sphere.md) and the [real projective space](real-projective-space.md) is that for the [sphere](sphere.md) all those points in the x-y circle are identified to a single point.

One more generalized argument of this is the [classification of closed surfaces](classification-of-closed-surfaces.md), in which the [real projective plane](real-projective-plane.md) is a [sphere](sphere.md) with a hole cut and one [Möbius strip](mobius-strip.md) glued in.
