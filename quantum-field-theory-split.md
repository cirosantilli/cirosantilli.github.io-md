# Quantum field theory

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic quantum mechanics](relativistic-quantum-mechanics-split.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Ciro Santilli's fetishes](ciro-santilli-s-fetishes.md)

Theoretical framework on which quantum field theories are based, theories based on framework include:
- [quantum electrodynamics](quantum-electrodynamics.md)
- [quantum chromodynamics](quantum-chromodynamics.md)
so basically the entire [Standard Model](standard-model-split.md)

The basic idea is that there is a field for each particle particle type.

E.g. in QED, one for the [electron](electron.md) and one for the [photon](photon-split.md): [https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/166709/are-electron-fields-and-photon-fields-part-of-the-same-field-in-qed](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/166709/are-electron-fields-and-photon-fields-part-of-the-same-field-in-qed).

And then those fields interact with some [Lagrangian](lagrangian.md).

One way to look at QFT is to split it into two parts:
- deriving the Lagrangians of the [Standard Model](standard-model-split.md): [S](s.md). This is the easier part, since the lagrangians themselves can be understood with not very advanced mathematics, and derived beautifully from symmetry constraints
- the qantization of fields. This is the hard part [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli-split.md) is unable to understand, TODO [mathematical formulation of quantum field theory](mathematical-formulation-of-quantum-field-theory.md).
Then interwined with those two is the part "OK, how to solve the equations, if they are solvable at all", which is an open problem: [Yang-Mills existence and mass gap](yang-mills-existence-and-mass-gap.md).

There appear to be two main equivalent formulations of quantum field theory:
- [second quantization](second-quantization.md)
- [path integral formulation](path-integral-formulation.md)
