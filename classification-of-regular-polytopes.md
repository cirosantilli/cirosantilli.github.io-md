# Classification of regular polytopes

↑ **Parent:** [Regular polytope](regular-polytope.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Classification (mathematics)](classification-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polytope#Classification_and_description)

The 3D regular convex polyhedrons are super famous, have the name: [Platonic solid](platonic-solid.md), and have been known since antiquity. In particular, there are only 5 of them.

The counts per dimension are:<a id="table-number-of-regular-polytopes-per-dimension"></a>


| Dimension | Count |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Infinite |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 6 |
| \>4 | 3 |

The cool thing is that the 3 that exist in 5+ dimensions are all of one of the three families:
- [simplex](simplex.md)
- [hypercube](hypercube.md)
- [cross polytope](cross-polytope.md)
Then, the 2 3D missing ones have 4D analogues and the sixth one in 4D does not have a 3D analogue: [the 24-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24-cell). Yes, this is the kind of irregular stuff [Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli-split.md) lives [for](the-beauty-of-mathematics.md).
