##### Immune system

↑ **Parent:** [List of anatomical systems](#list-of-anatomical-systems)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system)

A cool thought: [bacteria](taxonomy.md#bacteria) like [E. Coli](taxonomy.md#escherichia-coli) replicate every 20 minutes. A human replicates every 15 years. So how can [multicellular](taxonomy.md#multicellular-organism) beings possibly cope with the speed of evolution of parasites?

The answer is that within us, the [adaptive immune system](#adaptive-immune-system) is a population of cells that [evolves](#evolution) very quickly. So in a sense, within our bodies there is fast cell-level non-inheritable evolution happening daily!

#### Sequence alignment

↑ **Parent:** [Bioinformatics](#bioinformatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_alignment)

Sequence alignment is trying to match a [DNA](dna.md) or [amino acid](protein.md#amino-acid) sequence, even though the sequences might not be exactly the same, otherwise it would be a straight up [string-search algorithm](computer-science.md#string-search-algorithm).

This is fundamental in [bioinformatics](#bioinformatics) for two reasons:
- when you [sequence the DNA](dna.md#dna-sequencing) of a new [species](taxonomy.md#species), you can guess what each [protein](protein.md) does by comparing it with similar proteins in other species that you have already studied
- when doing [DNA sequencing](dna.md#dna-sequencing), and specially [short-read DNA sequencing](dna.md#short-read-dna-sequencing), you generally need to align the reads to reference genomes to know where you are inside the entire genome, and then be able to spot [mutations](dna.md#mutation), notably [single-nucleotide polymorphisms](dna.md#single-nucleotide-polymorphism)

##### BLAST (biotechnology)

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence alignment](#sequence-alignment)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLAST_(biotechnology))

BLAST is a [sequence alignment](#sequence-alignment).

The [NCBI](#national-center-for-biotechnology-information) free-to-use BLAST server: [https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi](https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). Having a centralized query server is fundamental, because the gain of [sequence alignment](#sequence-alignment) really comes from having one huge database to link information together, which is best centralized.
