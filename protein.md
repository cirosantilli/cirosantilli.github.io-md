## Model protein

↑ **Parent:** [Protein](protein.md)

[Ciro Santilli](ciro-santilli.md) defines a "[model protein](#model-protein)" as a protein which has been significantly used in the history of protein science, in analogy to the term [model organism](biology.md#model-organism).

Key characteristics of [model proteins](#model-protein) include:
- they are easy to obtain and are stable
- they are important to medical applications
- they are small and easier to understand for early studies

Important model proteins include:
- [insulin](#insulin): as a [peptide hormone](#peptide-hormone), this was small. Also it was useful and widely available even at pharmacies, [The Eighth Day of Creation](molecular-biology.md#the-eighth-day-of-creation) says you could get it a Boots, a major British pharmacy chain, and as such was a natural choice for the first sequencing by [Frederick Sanger](chemistry.md#frederick-sanger) published in 1951
- [hemoglobin](#hemoglobin)
- keratin

Binds an [amino acid](#amino-acid) to the correct corresponding [tRNA](cell.md#transfer-rna) sequence. [Wikipedia](website.md#wikipedia) mentions that humans have 20 of them, one for each [proteinogenic amino acid](#proteinogenic-amino-acid).

### Lysozyme

↑ **Parent:** [List of proteins](#list-of-proteins)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Enzyme](#enzyme), [Inate immune system](biology.md#innate-immune-system), [Protein](protein.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysozyme)

The [second protein to have its structure determined](#lysozyme-structure-resolution-1965), [after myoglobin](#myoglobin-structure-resolution-1958), by [X-ray crystallography](microscopy.md#x-ray-crystallography), in 1965.

Breaks up [peptidoglycan](#peptidoglycan) present in the [bacterial cell wall](taxonomy.md#bacterial-cell-wall), which is thicker in [Gram-positive bacteria](taxonomy.md#gram-positive-bacteria), which is what this [enzyme](#enzyme) seems to target.

Part of the [inate immune system](biology.md#innate-immune-system).

It is present on basically everything that [mammals](taxonomy.md#mammal) and [birds](taxonomy.md#bird) [excrete](molecular-biology.md#excretion), and it kills [bacteria](taxonomy.md#bacteria), both of which are reasons why it was discovered relatively early on.

#### Lysozyme structure resolution (1965)

↑ **Parent:** [Lysozyme](#lysozyme)

With [X-ray crystallography](microscopy.md#x-ray-crystallography) by David Chilton Phillips. The second [protein](protein.md) to be resolved fter [after myoglobin](#myoglobin-structure-resolution-1958), and the first [enzyme](#enzyme).

Published at: [Structure of Hen Egg-White Lysozyme: A Three-dimensional Fourier Synthesis at 2 Å Resolution (1965)](#structure-of-hen-egg-white-lysozyme-a-three-dimensional-fourier-synthesis-at-2-a-resolution-1965). The work was done while at the [Davy Faraday Research Laboratory](science.md#davy-faraday-research-laboratory) of the [Royal Institution](science.md#royal-institution).

Phillips also published a lower resolution (6[angstrom](system-of-units.md#angstrom)) of the enzyme-[inhibitor](#enzyme-inhibitor) complexes at about the same time: [Structure of Some Crystalline Lysozyme-Inhibitor Complexes Determined by X-Ray Analysis At 6 Å Resolution (1965)](#structure-of-some-crystalline-lysozyme-inhibitor-complexes-determined-by-x-ray-analysis-at-6-a-resolution-1965). The point of doing this is that it points out the [active site](#active-site) of the [enzyme](#enzyme).
