# Human Compatible

↑ **Parent:** [Artificial intelligence bibliography](artificial-intelligence-bibliography.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [AI alignment](ai-alignment.md), [Good book](good-book.md), [Implications of AGI](implications-of-agi.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_compatible)

The key takeaway is that setting an explicit [value function](value-function.md) to an [AGI](artificial-general-intelligence.md) entity is a good way to destroy the world due to poor [AI alignment](ai-alignment.md). We are more likely to not destroy by creating an AI whose goals is to "do want humans what it to do", but in a way that it does not know before hand what it is that humans want, and it has to learn from them. This approach appears to be known as [reward modeling](reward-modeling.md).

Some other cool ideas:
- a big thing that is missing for [AGI](artificial-general-intelligence.md) in the 2010's is some kind of more hierarchical representation of the continuous input data of the world, e.g.:
  - [intelligence is hierarchical](intelligence-is-hierarchical.md)
  - we can group continuous things into higher objects, e.g. all these pixels I'm seeing in front of me are a computer. So I treat all of them as a single object in my mind.
- [game theory](game-theory.md) can be seen as part of [artificial intelligence](artificial-intelligence-split.md) that deals with scenarios where multiple intelligent agents are involved
- [probability](probability.md) plays a crucial role in our everyday living, even though we don't think too much about it every explicitly. He gives a very good example of the cost/risk tradeoffs of planning to the airport to catch a plane. E.g.:
  - should you leave 2 days in advance to be sure you'll get there?
  - should you pay an armed escort to make sure you are not attacked in the way?
- [economy](economy-split.md), and notably the study of the [utility](utility.md), is intrinsically linked to [AI alignment](ai-alignment.md)
