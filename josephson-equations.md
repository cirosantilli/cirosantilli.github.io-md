# Josephson equations

↑ **Parent:** [Josephson effect](josephson-effect.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephson_equations)

Two equations derived [from first principles](from-first-principles.md) by [Brian Josephson](brian-josephson.md) that characterize the device, somewhat like an [I-V curve](current-voltage-characteristic.md):

$$
I(t) = I_c \sin (\varphi (t)) \\
\dv{\varphi(t)}{t} = \frac{2 e V(t)}{\hbar}
$$

where:
- $I_c$: [Josephson current](josephson-current.md)
- $\varphi$: the [Josephson phase](josephson-phase.md), a function $\R \to \R$ defined by the second equation plus initial conditions
- $V(t)$: input voltage of the system
- $I(t)$: current across the junction, determined by the input voltage

Note how these equations are not a typical [I-V curve](current-voltage-characteristic.md), as they are not an instantaneous dependency between voltage and current: the history of the voltage matters! Or in other words, the system has an internal state, represented by the [Josephson phase](josephson-phase.md) at a given point in time.

To understand them better, it is important to look at some important cases separately:
- [AC Josephson effect](ac-josephson-effect.md): V is a fixed [DC voltage](direct-current.md)
