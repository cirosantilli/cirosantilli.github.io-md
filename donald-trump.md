Trump's election was an act of protest by the people, because they felt, and rightly so, that the promises by the [democrats](democratic-party-united-states.md) to decrease [social inequality](social-inequality.md) were just full of [shit](feces.md).

So instead, the old voice of [nationalism](nationalism.md) spoke louder.

Ciro agrees with analysis of [Can't get you out of my head by Adam Curtis (2021)](can-t-get-you-out-of-my-head-by-adam-curtis-2021.md) that this is largely because [government is losing all power to do anything meaningful](governments-have-lost-all-power-to-companies.md). So the only thing left to do is to speak empty words to calm, or exacerbate, people's fears and hopes.

Ciro believes it is important not hate Trump and his believers, no matter how disgusting Trump might seem, a large part of which is likely theater. We have to try and understand them instead.

Trump's election shows clearly how the [democrats](democratic-party-united-states.md) let down the poor. This understanding is a good thing. It shows that we all have to make greater efforts to help the poor. Just voting for some random democrat candidate who doesn't really care every four years is not enough.

Another positive point of Trump's election is that it further highlighted the power of [social media](social-media.md) even further: it now feels more likely than ever before that anyone can run for office, since a president without any previous political office was elected (of course, being filthy reach helps a lot still, which is a problem). And this further highlights the need for regulate [social media](social-media.md), to prevent events such as [the deplatforming of Donald Trump](the-deplatforming-of-donald-trump.md)
