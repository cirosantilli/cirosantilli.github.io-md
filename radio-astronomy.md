# Radio astronomy

↑ **Parent:** [Astronomy](astronomy-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_astronomy)

[Radio astronomy](radio-astronomy.md) is cool because it revealed:
- some very interesting new types of [astronomical objects](astronomical-object.md) that were not as noticeable in the [visible spectrum](visible-spectrum.md) notably:
  - [quasars](quasar.md): quasars are extremely [](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/redshifted), which means by [Hubble's law](hubble-s-law.md) that they are very far from [Earth](earth.md), so the fact that we could see them at all meant they must have produced immense amounts of light
  - [pulsars](pulsar.md): scientists thought they had found [extraterrestrial life](extraterrestrial-life.md) when they saw these regularly pulsating signal sources!
- [cosmic microwave background](cosmic-microwave-background.md) which is a major evidence for the [Big Bang](big-bang.md)
- radio wavelengths penetrate [Earth's atmosphere](atmosphere-of-earth.md) better than the [visible spectrum](visible-spectrum.md) making it easier to make ground-based observations
The [1974 Nobel Prize in Physics](1974-nobel-prize-in-physics.md) was awarded for pioneering [radio astronomy](radio-astronomy.md) from the late 40s onwards done at the [University of Cambridge](university-of-cambridge.md) which was an epicenter of early research in that area, leading to the creation of the [Mullard Radio Astronomy Observatory](mullard-radio-astronomy-observatory.md) in 1958.
