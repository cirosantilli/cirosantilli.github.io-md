# Angular momentum operator

↑ **Parent:** [Uncertainty principle](uncertainty-principle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_momentum_operator)

Basically the operators are just analogous to the classical ones e.g. the classical:

$$
L_z = x p_y - y p_x
$$

becomes:

$$
\hat{L}_z = -i \hbar \left( x\pdv{}{y} - y\pdv{}{x} \right)
$$

Besides the angular momentum in each direction, we also have the [total angular momentum](total-angular-momentum-operator.md):

$$
\hat{L}^2 = \hat{L}_x + \hat{L}_y + \hat{L}_z
$$

Then you have to understand what each one of those does to the each [atomic orbital](atomic-orbital.md):
- total angular momentum: determined by the [azimuthal quantum number](azimuthal-quantum-number.md)
- angular momentum in one direction ($z$ by convention): determined by the [magnetic quantum number](magnetic-quantum-number.md)

There is an [uncertainty principle](uncertainty-principle.md) between the x, y and z angular momentums, we can only measure one of them with certainty at a time. [Video 19. "Quantum Mechanics 7a - Angular Momentum I by ViaScience (2013)"](#video-quantum-mechanics-7a-angular-momentum-i-by-viascience-2013) justifies this intuitively by mentioning that this is analogous to [precession](precession.md): if you try to measure electrons e.g. with the [Zeeman effect](zeeman-effect.md) the precess on the other directions which you end up modifing.
