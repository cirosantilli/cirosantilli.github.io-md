<h1 id="why-can-t-you-collimate-incoherent-light-as-well-as-a-laser">Why can't you collimate incoherent light as well as a laser?</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Lasers vs other light sources](lasers-vs-other-light-sources.md)

[https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/252393/why-cant-incoherent-light-be-collimated-as-well-as-laser-light-e-g-in-a-laser](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/252393/why-cant-incoherent-light-be-collimated-as-well-as-laser-light-e-g-in-a-laser)

You could put an [LED](light-emitting-diode.md) in a cavity with a thin long hole but then, most rays, which are not aligned with the hole, will just bounce inside forever producing heat.

So you would have a very hot device, and very little efficiency on the light output. This heat might also behave like a [black-body radiation](black-body-radiation.md) source, so you would not have a single frequency.

The beauty of lasers is the laser cavity (two parallel mirrors around the medium) selects parallel motion preferentially, see e.g.: [https://youtu.be/_JOchLyNO_w?t=832](https://youtu.be/_JOchLyNO_w?t=832) from [Video 31. "How Lasers Work by Scientized (2017)"](laser.md#video-how-lasers-work-by-scientized-2017)
