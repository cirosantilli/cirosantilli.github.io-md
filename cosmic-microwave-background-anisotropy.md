# Cosmic microwave background anisotropy

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic microwave background](cosmic-microwave-background.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [2006 Nobel Prize in Physics](2006-nobel-prize-in-physics.md)

There is a slight variation in temperature of [CMB](cosmic-microwave-background.md) across the sky of the order of 200 microKelvin. It is small to the ~2.7 K average temperature, but it can be measured.

If the [initial conditions](initial-condition.md) of the [Big Bang](big-bang.md) and the [laws of physics](law-of-physics.md) were perfectly symmetric, then we could expect the [universe](universe.md) to just be one perfectly uniform boring soup.

But instead some asymetry made all the fun weird things we see today happen eventually, like [galaxies](galaxy.md) and [life](life.md).

And the [cosmic microwave background](cosmic-microwave-background.md) serves as a way for us to look back in time to the early conditions of the universe, as it was set in stone as soon as the universe became transparent to this [light](light.md) during [recombination](recombination-cosmology.md).

Or if you want to get poetic, it is the closest we can ever get to listening to the original word of [God](god.md) when he setup the [initial conditions](initial-condition.md) of the universe.

The ansiotropies of CMB is the ultimate astronomical compass we will ever have, as it is the thing with the least [proper motion](proper-motion.md).
