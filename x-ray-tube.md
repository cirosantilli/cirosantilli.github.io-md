<a id="video-william-coolidge-explains-medical-imaging-and-x-rays-1940"></a>
**[Video 17](#video-william-coolidge-explains-medical-imaging-and-x-rays-1940). William Coolidge explains medical imaging and X-rays (1940).** [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j314IQ0-pJk). Video sponsored by [General Electric](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/general-electric). A cool insight of this video is that a hot [cathode](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/cathode) is a more reliable electron source. Previous systems, and presumably including the discovery of [X-rays](x-ray.md), leftover gas in the tube was used. But this makes things more difficult to control, as we also want to remove as much gas as possible from the vacuum, otherwise electrons collide with the gas and lose energy before hitting the anode.

<a id="video-how-does-x-ray-tube-works-by-biomedengg"></a>
**[Video 18](#video-how-does-x-ray-tube-works-by-biomedengg). How Does X ray Tube Works by BiomedEngg.** [Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3_bZCA7tlFQ). Describes in particular the rotating [cathode](https://ourbigbook.com/go/topic/cathode) method. Interesting observation that this is especially important since the cathode cannot cool quickly due to the vacuum.
