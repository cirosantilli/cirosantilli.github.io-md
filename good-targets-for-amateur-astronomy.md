# Good targets for amateur astronomy

↑ **Parent:** [Amateur astronomy](amateur-astronomy.md)

Looking at most [astronomical object](astronomical-object.md) through a [telescope](telescope.md) is boring because you only see a white ball or point every time. Such targets would likely only be interesting with [spectroscopy](spectroscopy.md) analysis.

There are however some objects that you can see the structure of even with an [amateur telescope](amateur-telescope.md), and that makes them very exciting.

Some good ones:
- [The Moon](moon.md), notably crater detail.
- [Saturn](saturn.md). Clearly visible to the naked eye, but looks like a ball. But under an amateur telescope, you can clearly see that there is a disk. Clearly discerning that the disk is a ring, i.e. seeing the gap, is a bit harder though.
- [Jupiter](jupiter.md). Clearly visible to the naked eye, it is quite huge. The four [Galilean moons](galilean-moons.md), being [Earth](earth.md)-sized, are incredibly clearly visible, tested on [Celestron NexStar 4SE](celestron-nexstar-4se.md) 25mm/9mm eyepiece. Colored gas clouds are hard though, you will likely just see it bright white. [https://www.reddit.com/r/telescopes/comments/35xrbb/how_can_i_see_the_color_of_jupiter_with_my/](https://www.reddit.com/r/telescopes/comments/35xrbb/how_can_i_see_the_color_of_jupiter_with_my/)
- a [double star](double-star.md). As mentioned at [https://www.relativelyinteresting.com/10-astronomical-targets-new-telescope/](https://www.relativelyinteresting.com/10-astronomical-targets-new-telescope/) [Albireo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albireo) are incredibly separated. Also it is is easy to find manually being in a major well known constellation. It is no wonder it is not quite even known if they are gravitationally bound or not!
- [Andromeda Galaxy](andromeda-galaxy.md). This is when things start getting hard. You can see a faint cloud, but it is not super clear that it has a center.

  One important understanding is that [it is not possible to see stars outside of the Milky Way by naked eye](it-is-not-possible-to-see-stars-outside-of-the-milky-way-by-naked-eye.md).

  It is at this point that you start to learn that pictures of faint objects require longer term exposure and averaging of the images taken. For this you need:
  - a digital camera attached to the scope
  - a computerized scope that slowly moves to track the point of interest
  - image processing software that does the averaging
  Just looking through the scope to immediately see something is not enough.

  [Video 10. "Andromeda Galaxy with only a Camera, Lens, & Tripod by Nebula Photos (2020)"](andromeda-galaxy.md#video-andromeda-galaxy-with-only-a-camera-lens-and-tripod-by-nebula-photos-2020) gives a good notion of expectation adjustment.
