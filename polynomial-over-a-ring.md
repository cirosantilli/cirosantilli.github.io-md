# Polynomial over a ring

↑ **Parent:** [Domain of a polynomial](domain-of-a-polynomial.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_over_a_ring)

The usual definition of a [polynomial](polynomial.md) is over a [field](field-mathematics.md) as shown at [polynomial over a field](polynomial-over-a-field.md).

However, there is nothing in the immediate definition that prevents us from having a [ring](ring-mathematics.md) instead, i.e. a [field](field-mathematics.md) but without the [commutative property](commutative-property.md) and [inverse elements](inverse-element.md).

The only thing is that then we would need to differentiate between different orderings of the terms of [multivariate polynomial](multivariate-polynomial.md), e.g. the following would all be potentially different terms:

$$
2xxy + 2xyx + 2yxx +
x2xy + x2yx + y2xx +
xx2y + xy2x + yx2x +
xxy2 + xyx2 + yxx2
$$

while for a field they would all go into a single term:

$$
12x^2y
$$

so when considering a polynomial over a [ring](ring-mathematics.md) we end up with a lot more more possible terms.

If the [ring](ring-mathematics.md) is a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) however, polynomials do look like proper polynomials: [Section "Polynomial over a commutative ring"](polynomial-over-a-commutative-ring.md).
