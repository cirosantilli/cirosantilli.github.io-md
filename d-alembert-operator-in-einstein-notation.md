<h1 id="d-alembert-operator-in-einstein-notation">d'Alembert operator in Einstein notation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Laplacian in Einstein notation](laplacian-in-einstein-notation.md)

Given the function $\psi$:

$$
\psi : \R^4 \to \C
$$

the operator can be written in [Planck units](planck-units.md) as:

$$
\partial_i \partial^i \psi(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3) - m^2 \psi(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3) = 0
$$

often written without function arguments as:

$$
\partial_i \partial^i \psi
$$

Note how this looks just like the [Laplacian in Einstein notation](laplacian-in-einstein-notation.md),  since the [d'Alembert operator](d-alembert-operator.md) is just a generalization of the [laplace operator](laplace-operator.md) to [Minkowski space](minkowski-space.md).
